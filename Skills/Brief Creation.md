# Brief Creation

## Purpose
A brief is the operational directive that transforms strategy into creative execution. Briefs tell your team exactly what to create, why, how it should feel, and what success looks like. A clear brief prevents scope creep, reduces revisions, and aligns everyone on the vision.

**Core Philosophy:** A good brief = 80% of the battle. Clarity upfront saves chaos in post.

**Vocabulary (adopted 2026-10-07, see [[Content Framework Method]]):** every brief line item carries four tags: **Pillar · Bucket · Job · Series (Format)**. Pillars are what we talk about (products, services, audiences, themes; 6–24 months). Buckets are what the content does and never rename: Awareness, Desire, Story, Choice. Jobs are the piece's intent: Educate, Entertain, Inspire, Empathize, Prove, Convert. A shoot-day grouping in a production brief is a *segment*. The word "bucket" is reserved for the four above.

---

## Complete Brief Hierarchy at Launchpad

There are **multiple brief types**, each serving a distinct purpose in the content production workflow:

### 1. **Campaign Brief** (Strategic Foundation)
**When:** At the start of any campaign or project
**Purpose:** Establish overall business goals, audience, messaging strategy, and expected outcomes
**Audience:** Client, internal leadership, creative team
**Typical Length:** 2–3 pages
**Key Sections:** Business objective, audiences, messaging framework, success metrics, timeline

**Example:** "Blue Mountain Energy Spring Lead Generation Campaign" – Strategy for layered ad campaigns across Meta + Google

---

### 2. **Content Strategy Brief** (Planning)
**When:** Before content creation starts (for large projects or multiple pieces)
**Purpose:** Outline content calendar, themes, messaging angles, and content mix
**Audience:** Creative team, content creators
**Typical Length:** 1–2 pages
**Key Sections:** Theme (if any), pillar mix, audience segmentation, posting cadence, tagged line items (Pillar · Bucket · Job · Series)

**Example:** "Upbeat Health March Graphics Brief" – 24 graphics across wound care and primary care with specific themes

---

### 3. **Production/Shoot Brief** (Execution Blueprint)
**When:** For any production day (photo, video, interview shoot)
**Purpose:** Give the crew clarity on what's being shot, when, where, and how
**Audience:** Camera crew, director, talent, production manager
**Typical Length:** 2–4 pages
**Key Sections:** Objective, shoot segments (grouped by pillar, each series tagged with bucket and job), location, talent, gear, schedule, style direction

**Example:** "Taco Bell Cantina Media Day Brief" – Multi-skit shoot covering 11 different content pieces

---

### 4. **Shot List** (Tactical Specificity)
**When:** Attached to or part of production brief
**Purpose:** Break down exact shots/angles needed for the shoot
**Audience:** Camera crew, director, editor
**Typical Length:** 1–3 pages
**Key Sections:** Talking heads, B-roll, photos, skits, drone shots with checkboxes

**Example:** "BME Shot List – Talking Heads + Process B-Roll"

---

### 5. **Creative Brief** (Content Direction)
**When:** For individual pieces (single video, carousel, graphic, ad)
**Purpose:** Guide creative direction, messaging, and visual style for one deliverable
**Audience:** Designer, video creator, copywriter
**Typical Length:** 1–2 pages
**Key Sections:** Objective, audience, tone, visual direction, messaging, deliverable specs

**Example:** "Upbeat Health March Graphics Brief – Still #3: Healing Story"

---

### 6. **Graphic Design Brief** (Visual Specification)
**When:** For any graphic design project
**Purpose:** Provide art direction, color palette, imagery style, messaging, and specs
**Audience:** Graphic designer, brand team
**Typical Length:** 2–3 pages
**Key Sections:** Brand context, visual direction, content breakdown, technical specs, moodboard

**Example:** "Upbeat Health Moodboard + Design Direction" for all 24 monthly graphics

---

### 7. **Video Editing Brief** (Post-Production)
**When:** After footage is captured, before editor starts cutting
**Purpose:** Clarify style, pacing, color grade, music, and technical requirements
**Audience:** Video editor, post-production team
**Typical Length:** 2–4 pages
**Key Sections:** Overview, style direction, video-by-video breakdown, footage organization, technical specs

**Example:** "Taco Bell Skit Editing Brief" – Direction for 4 comedic skits with pacing/music/tone

---

### 8. **Ad Campaign Brief** (Paid Advertising)
**When:** Before launching any paid ad campaign
**Purpose:** Outline campaign strategy, audiences, creative assets, budget allocation, and success metrics
**Audience:** Media buyer, creative team, client
**Typical Length:** 3–5 pages
**Key Sections:** Campaign goals, audiences, creative assets, platform strategy, budget, timeline

**Example:** "Blue Mountain Energy – Ad Campaign Plan" – Layered funnel strategy for Meta + Google

---

### 9. **Media Day Brief** (Comprehensive All-in-One)
**When:** For complex shoot days with multiple content types
**Purpose:** Combines production + creative guidance in one place
**Audience:** Full crew, creative team, client
**Typical Length:** 4–6 pages
**Key Sections:** Project goal, shoot segments by pillar, shot list, talent, locations, gear, style, timeline, post-production

**Example:** "Ben Nadig Law Media Day Brief" – Full day of shooting talking heads, reactions, and skits

---

### 10. **Post-Production/Deliverables Brief** (Final Output)
**When:** Before final approval/delivery to client
**Purpose:** Specify final format, resolution, color space, delivery method, approval workflow
**Audience:** Post-production team, client approval person
**Typical Length:** 1 page checklist
**Key Sections:** Deliverable formats, technical specs, approval path, final delivery location

## The Brief Creation Process (Step-by-Step)

### Before Creating a Brief

1. **Understand the Business Goal**
   - What does the client want to achieve? (leads, awareness, sales, engagement)
   - What's the timeline? (weeks vs. months)
   - What's the budget/resources available?

2. **Know the Audience**
   - Who are we speaking to?
   - What problems do they have?
   - Where are they (geography, platform, context)?

3. **Clarify Deliverables**
   - How many pieces? (1 video or 10 graphics?)
   - What platforms? (TikTok vs. LinkedIn changes everything)
   - What format? (vertical video vs. carousel)

---

### How to Write a Clear Brief

**Rule 1: Be Specific**
- ❌ "Make it punchy"
- ✅ "Use fast cuts every 1–2 seconds and high-energy music"

**Rule 2: Show, Don't Tell**
- ❌ "Professional looking"
- ✅ [Include 3 reference videos that match the vibe]

**Rule 3: Answer the "Why"**
- Not just WHAT: "Create a testimonial video"
- Include WHY: "Create a testimonial video to build trust with cold audiences skeptical of other HVAC companies"

**Rule 4: Use Templates Consistently**
- All briefs should follow the same structure (creative team knows where to find info)
- Make templates editable, not just fill-in-the-blank

**Rule 5: Clarify Constraints Early**
- Budget limits
- Timeline/deadline
- Approval workflow
- Revision limits
- File format/technical specs

---

### Common Mistakes When Creating Briefs

1. **Vague direction:** "Make it look good" (too subjective)
2. **Missing references:** Expecting team to guess style without examples
3. **No context:** Not explaining business goal or target audience
4. **Too many stakeholders:** Multiple people giving conflicting feedback
5. **Approval chaos:** No clear sign-off person or process
6. **Scope creep:** Adding new requests mid-project without adjusting timeline
7. **Last-minute specs:** Saying "needs to be vertical" after editor starts cutting
8. **No linkage:** Not explaining how individual pieces fit into larger campaign

---

### Brief as Accountability Tool

A good brief also serves as a **checklist for completion**:

- Did we hit the business goal?
- Does it match the target audience?
- Did we deliver all assets on time?
- Did we stay within budget?
- Is quality aligned with spec?
- Can we show ROI or measure success?

If any of these aren't covered in the brief, you don't have clarity.

## Creative Brief Template

Use this for content, campaigns, or single-piece direction:

```
# 🎯 CREATIVE BRIEF

**Project:** [Project Name]
**Client:** [Client Name]
**Deliverable:** [Video, Photo Set, Carousel, etc.]
**Due Date:** [Date]
**Assigned To:** [Team Member]

---

## 1. OBJECTIVE

**What are we making?**
[One-sentence description]

**Why are we making it?**
[Business goal or campaign objective]

---

## 2. BRIEF

**What's the core idea or angle?**
[The creative concept/hook]

**What's the tone?**
[Professional, humorous, urgent, heartfelt, educational, etc.]

**What should it FEEL like?**
[Emotional resonance or vibe]

---

## 3. KEY MESSAGING

**Primary message:**
[Main point we're communicating]

**Secondary messages:**
- [Message 2]
- [Message 3]

**What should NOT be in this?**
[Anything to avoid, sensitivities, off-limits topics]

---

## 4. AUDIENCE

**Who is this for?**
[Specific audience segment]

**What do they care about?**
[Their pain points, desires, motivations]

**Where will they see this?**
[Platform, context, how they'll discover it]

---

## 5. DELIVERABLES

**Format:**
[Video, photo set, carousel, etc.]

**Specs:**
- Length: [duration or dimensions]
- Aspect ratio: [vertical, horizontal, square]
- Platform: [TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, etc.]
- Any technical requirements: [resolution, captions, music licensing, etc.]

**How many?**
[1 video, 3 photos, etc.]

---

## 6. VISUAL DIRECTION

**Style:**
[Minimalist, maximalist, cinematic, raw, polished, etc.]

**Color palette:**
[Brand colors or mood board reference]

**Props/wardrobe:**
[Specific items or general direction]

**Location/setting:**
[Where will this be shot?]

**Key visuals to include:**
[Product, logo, branded elements]

---

## 7. CALL TO ACTION

**Primary CTA:**
[What should viewer do?]

**How it appears:**
[On-screen text, voice-over, graphic, etc.]

---

## 8. REFERENCE / INSPIRATION

**What have we made before that felt right?**
[Link to previous project]

**Competitor or external examples:**
[Links to inspiration]

**Do NOT do this:**
[References of what to avoid]

---

## 9. NOTES FOR CREATOR/TEAM

[Any special instructions, sensitivities, approval workflows, etc.]

---

## APPROVAL

- [ ] Client approved?
- [ ] Internally aligned?
- [ ] Legal review (if needed)?

Date approved: [Date]
Approved by: [Name]

## Real-World Examples

### Example 1: Social Media Post Brief

```
# 🎯 CREATIVE BRIEF

**Project:** Carnival Home Winter Campaign
**Deliverable:** Instagram Carousel (5 slides)
**Due Date:** March 15, 2026
**Assigned To:** Arija (Designer)

---

## OBJECTIVE

Showcase Carnival Home's luxury bedding and robes as the ultimate cozy-season upgrade. Drive traffic to product page and build email list.

---

## TONE

Aspirational but warm. "Treat yourself" energy without being salesy.

---

## KEY MESSAGING

- Sleep like you're still on a cruise
- Luxury doesn't have to break the bank
- This is self-care

---

## AUDIENCE

Women, 35–55, interested in home goods and wellness. Browse Instagram while relaxing.

---

## DELIVERABLES

**Format:** Instagram Carousel (5 slides)
**Aspect ratio:** 1080 x 1350 (vertical)
**Copy length:** 100–150 words per slide

---

## VISUAL DIRECTION

**Slide 1:** Hero image of folded Carnival Home sheets with soft lighting
**Slide 2:** Lifestyle shot (person wrapping in robe)
**Slide 3:** Flat lay of products
**Slide 4:** Close-up texture shot (showing quality)
**Slide 5:** CTA slide with product link and discount code

**Color palette:** Warm neutrals, soft blues, touches of gold

---

## CTA

Primary: "Shop the collection — link in bio"
Secondary: "Save 15% with code CRUISE15"

---

## REFERENCE

[Link to previous successful carousel]

Don't do: harsh lighting, overly product-focused (feels like infomercial)
```

```

**Project:** Taco Bell Cantina Social Content Capture
**Client:** DRG (Diversified Restaurant Group)
**Shoot Date:** March 15, 2026

- Food plating and serving
- Crowd reactions
- Nightclub ambiance (lighting, energy)

- Cocktail hero shots (6)

## TALENT

- Kyle Fuller (lead talent) — casual attire, energy

## LOCATION LOGISTICS

**Address:** [Cantina address]
**Parking:** [Details]

## GEAR

- Gimbal (for walk-and-talk skits)
- A7RV (still photos)

## STYLE

**Skit 1 (Dollar Bill Trap)**

- Fast jump-cuts between drink, menu, events shots
- Ending: Kyle + Haley walking downstairs
- Tone: comedic, high-energy pitch

**B-Roll (Cocktails)**
- Bartender pouring

- Pour from above
- Sip/reaction shot if possible

**Photos**
- Golden hour shots if possible (sunset time)
- Highlight bright colors and food appeal
- Include both product + people for lifestyle feel

## APPROVALS

- [ ] DRG approved shot list
- [ ] Talent confirmed availability
- [ ] Cantina access confirmed
- [ ] Gear checklist complete

Brief finalized: March 10, 2026
Approved by: Anthony Mendoza

## Brief Best Practices

1. **Be Specific:** "Fast-paced, energetic cuts" > "Make it punchy"
2. **Use Visuals:** Include mood boards, reference links, color palettes
3. **Answer the "Why":** Team should understand the business goal, not just the task
4. **One Clear Outcome:** What does done look like?
5. **Constraint Clarity:** Timeline, budget, approval workflows
6. **Version Control:** Date your briefs; reference versions when revising
7. **Feedback Loop:** When brief changes, update everyone immediately
