# LP University Course Creation Process

**Maintained By:** Anthony Mendoza + AI Instructional Designers
**Last Updated:** April 4, 2026
**Purpose:** Step-by-step process for how LaunchPad creates courses (from initial concept through launch)

---

## OVERVIEW: The LaunchPad Course Creation Workflow

LaunchPad doesn't create courses in isolation. Courses emerge from real client work, team expertise, and proven frameworks. Here's the process:

1. **Validate the topic** (Does LaunchPad do this? Do students need this?)
2. **Outline the curriculum** (Module-by-module structure)
3. **Record the instructor video** (Descript-powered, conversational)
4. **Transcribe and edit** (Descript does transcription, AI edits for clarity)
5. **Create supporting assets** (Worksheets, templates, checklists)
6. **Design the course** (Platform build-out, module structure)
7. **Test and iterate** (Live testing with first cohort)
8. **Launch and promote** (Email, ads, social, community)
9. **Gather feedback and improve** (Ongoing optimization)

---

## PHASE 1: COURSE CONCEPT & VALIDATION

### Step 1.1: Identify the Topic
**Source:** Comes from one of these places:
- Core work LaunchPad does with clients (content, ads, CRM, strategy, etc.)
- Questions students/prospects ask repeatedly
- Gaps in current curriculum
- New process LaunchPad has developed
- Team member expertise (Kyle on content, Amber on systems, etc.)

**Example:**
"Kyle Fuller is a master of content creation. Students ask constantly how he gets so many views. This should be a course."

### Step 1.2: Validate Demand
**Questions to Ask:**
- Do prospects/students ask about this repeatedly?
- Is this something LaunchPad does daily?
- Is there a clear before/after transformation?
- Can we teach this in modules (not just 1 video)?
- Do we have a practitioner who can teach it authentically?

**Output:** Decision to proceed or shelve the idea

### Step 1.3: Define Learning Outcomes
**Format:**
"By the end of this course, students can:
- [Skill 1]
- [Skill 2]
- [Skill 3]
- [Skill 4]"

**Example (Chapter 2 - Content Creation):**
"By the end, students can:
- Research content intentionally (reverse-engineer high-performers)
- Plan shoots efficiently (shot lists, briefs, timelines)
- Capture video with minimal equipment
- Edit for maximum impact (hook-first, pacing, platform optimization)
- Optimize for each platform"

### Step 1.4: Outline Module Structure
**Template:**
- Module 1: [Topic] — [30-50 min]
- Module 2: [Topic] — [30-50 min]
- Module 3: [Topic] — [30-50 min]
- etc.

**Key Principle:** Each module teaches one thing well. Not overwhelming. Builds on previous module.

**Example (Chapter 1 - Strategy):**
- Module 1: Quarterly Planning Setup (45 min)
- Module 2: Choose Your Objective (30 min)
- Module 3: Define Your Audience (50 min)
- Module 4: Message Strategy (45 min)
- Module 5: Systems & Metrics (40 min)
- Module 6: Channel Strategy (35 min)
- Module 7: Final Alignment Check (30 min)

## PHASE 2: CURRICULUM DEVELOPMENT

### Step 2.1: Write the Module Outline (AI-Assisted)
**Process:**
1. Meet with instructor (30-60 min video call)
2. AI takes notes on:
   - Core concept
   - Key teaching points
   - Real examples they use
   - Common student misconceptions
   - Action steps (what students should do)
3. AI writes outline based on conversation
4. Instructor reviews and refines

**Output:** Detailed outline with:
- Module title
- Learning objectives
- 3-5 key concepts
- 2-3 real examples
- 2-4 action steps

### Step 2.2: Create the Module Structure Template
**Every Module Has:**

**Overview (2-3 min read)**
- What this module is about
- Why it matters
- How it connects to previous modules

**Core Concept (1-2 paragraphs)**
- The main idea
- Why it's different/important
- Hook or reframe

**Breakdown (Main Content)**
- Detailed explanation
- Examples (2-3 real examples, not generic)
- Common mistakes
- How to think about it

**Action Steps**
- Concrete tasks before moving forward (3-5 specific steps)
- Not optional; students should do these

**Worksheet Guidance**
- Which fields on the worksheet to complete
- What output students should create
- Connected to the action steps

**Key Takeaways**
- 3-5 bullet points summarizing the lesson
- Reinforce the core concept

### Step 2.3: Develop Supporting Assets
**For Each Module, Create:**

**Worksheet (PDF + Notion)**
- Directly mirrors the lesson
- Fields for students to fill in
- Pretty but functional
- Downloadable

**Checklist**
- Step-by-step action items
- Checkbox format
- Print-friendly

**One-Pager (Visual Summary)**
- 1-page visual breakdown of key concept
- Infographic style
- Shareable

**Script/Template (If Applicable)**
- Email template
- SMS template
- Copy template
- Ad copy framework

**Resource List**
- Tools mentioned in the module
- Inspiration sources
- Links to further learning

## PHASE 3: VIDEO RECORDING & TRANSCRIPTION

### Step 3.1: Prepare the Instructor
**Before Recording:**
- Send them the module outline (they'll talk conversationally to it, not read it)
- No script needed (conversational is better than memorized)
- They can refer to outline, but should speak naturally
- Encourage stories and real examples
- Mention this is raw footage—editing happens later

**Best Practices:**
- Find quiet location (home office, studio)
- Simple background (not distracting)
- Good lighting (natural or simple ring light)
- Lavalier mic (better audio than built-in mic)
- Record in Descript (Descript has built-in recording + transcription)

### Step 3.2: Record the Module Video (Descript Native)
**Process:**
1. Instructor opens Descript on their computer
2. Records directly in Descript (screen recording + audio)
3. Records 15-50 minutes of content (teaching the module)
4. Descript auto-transcribes as they speak

**Descript Advantages:**
- Built-in transcription (real-time or after)
- Edit transcript = edit video
- Remove filler words ("um," "like") automatically
- Add text overlays to video
- Export video or transcript separately
- Cloud-based (accessible anywhere)

**Tips for Recording:**
- Speak clearly and slowly (transcription works better)
- Pause between ideas (easier for transcript editing)
- Repeat key points (reinforces in both video and transcript)
- Tell stories (more engaging, transcripts read well)
- No script (talk naturally, like teaching someone 1-on-1)

### Step 3.3: Clean Up Transcription
**Process (AI-Assisted):**
1. Descript auto-transcribes the video
2. AI reviews transcript for:
   - Filler word removal (um, like, you know, so, right)
   - Clarity (does it read well?)
   - Structure (does it follow module outline?)
   - Examples (are they clear?)
   - Accuracy (did Descript capture everything correctly?)
3. AI creates cleaned transcript
4. Instructor reviews for accuracy
5. AI formats for reading (headers, bullets, paragraphs)

**Edited Transcript Should:**
- Read smoothly (as if written, not transcribed)
- Maintain instructor's voice (don't over-edit personality)
- Have clear headers and structure
- Include examples and stories
- Be searchable and skimmable
- Be 2,000-4,000 words (module-length)

## PHASE 4: VIDEO EDITING & OPTIMIZATION

### Step 4.1: Edit the Video (Descript)
**Process:**
1. Load transcript into Descript
2. Remove dead air, long pauses, filler words
3. Add text overlays (key points, quotes)
4. Add chapter markers (for chapters/timestamps)
5. Trim total length to 15-50 minutes
6. Color correct (if needed)
7. Export video file

**Editing Guidelines:**
- Keep it conversational (some pauses are natural)
- Remove only obvious dead air and filler
- Add text to reinforce key points (not too much)
- Chapter markers every 5-10 minutes (helps navigation)
- Total length: Varies by module (30-45 min is sweet spot)

### Step 4.2: Create Supporting Media
**Slides/Thumbnails:**
- Extract key quotes from transcript
- Create 1-2 social media graphics per module
- Create 1 thumbnail for YouTube/email
- Create 1-2 carousel graphics for LinkedIn

**Video Clips:**
- Extract 30-60 second clips for social media
- Create 3-5 short clips per module video
- Add captions to clips

## PHASE 5: DESIGN & BUILD COURSE PLATFORM

### Step 5.1: Choose Platform
**LaunchPad Options:**
- Kajabi (all-in-one, pricey, very good)
- Teachable (good for courses, moderate price)
- Circle (community-first, moderate price)
- Custom build (complex, expensive, full control)
- Notion + email (DIY, free/cheap, limited features)

**Current LaunchPad Approach:**
Likely using a combination:
- GoHighLevel for enrollment funnel + CRM integration
- Dedicated course platform (Kajabi or Teachable) for content delivery
- Slack community for peer support
- Email for module drip-out

### Step 5.2: Structure Course in Platform
**Module Setup:**
1. Module title and description
2. Video embedded (with transcript below)
3. Downloadable worksheet (PDF + Notion link)
4. Checklist (downloadable)
5. One-pager (downloadable)
6. Next module link (progression)

**Access Control:**
- Free modules: Chapter 1, Module 1 (all can access)
- Paid modules: Require enrollment to access
- Cohort-based: Unlock module-by-module on schedule (not all at once)

### Step 5.3: Design Email Drip Sequence
**Automated Emails:**
- Welcome email (when they enroll)
- Module 1 welcome + login link
- 48 hours: "Have you started?"
- Day 7: "You've got this"
- Day 14: "Time for Module 2" (if following schedule)
- Low engagement: Re-engagement email at Day 21

**Content of Emails:**
- Encourage action
- Remind of benefits
- Share student wins (social proof)
- Link to community (Slack channel)
- Clear next steps

## PHASE 6: TESTING & ITERATION

### Step 6.1: Launch Beta Cohort
**Process:**
- Invite 20-30 internal + trusted external students
- Free or heavily discounted access
- Explicit feedback requested
- 4-6 week timeline
- Weekly feedback calls with group

**Feedback to Gather:**
- Was the module clear?
- Did the action steps work?
- Was the worksheet useful?
- Was the length right?
- Did you get the promised result?
- What was confusing?
- What was missing?

### Step 6.2: Iterate Based on Feedback
**Common Findings:**
- Module too long → split into 2 modules
- Worksheet confusing → redesign it
- Concept not clear → re-record that section
- Action steps too hard → add mini-step
- Missing example → add case study
- Students wanted more depth → add optional deep-dive module

**Update Process:**
1. Collect feedback (surveys, calls, Slack)
2. Identify patterns (not every suggestion is valid)
3. Make updates to transcript, worksheet, video
4. Re-export assets
5. Upload updated version
6. Notify beta students of improvements

### Step 6.3: Gather Social Proof
**During Beta:**
- Get 3-5 testimonial videos (30-60 sec each)
- Ask: "What was most valuable?" + "How will you use this?"
- Ask permission to use their name/face in marketing
- Document specific results (if any)

**Output:** 3-5 testimonial videos for marketing

## PHASE 7: LAUNCH & PROMOTION

### Step 7.1: Create Launch Campaign
**Email Sequence (Pre-Launch):**
- Day 0: Teaser (something big coming)
- Day 3: What it is + why (reveal)
- Day 5: Benefits + proof (what you'll get)
- Day 7: How to enroll (last chance mentality)

**Social Media:**
- TikTok/Reels: 3-5 short clips from course
- LinkedIn: Thought leadership (why this matters)
- Twitter: Quick takes + hot takes
- Facebook: Testimonial videos + stats

**Paid Ads:**
- Facebook/Instagram: Testimonial videos + pain point messaging
- YouTube: Pre-roll ads on related content
- LinkedIn: Thought leadership + credibility

### Step 7.2: Email Launch Sequence
**Day 1 (Launch):**
- Subject: "LaunchPad University [Course Title] is live"
- Body: What it is, who it's for, enroll button

**Day 3:**
- Social proof (testimonial or stat)
- Reminder to enroll

**Day 7:**
- Last chance messaging
- Early access bonus ending

**Day 14 (Post-Launch):**
- Stories from first students
- Proof of value (results)

### Step 7.3: Ongoing Promotion
**Weekly:**
- Share student wins in community
- Post social media clip
- Send email to waitlist (social proof)

**Monthly:**
- Student testimonial
- New promo campaign
- Update course based on student feedback

## PHASE 8: ONGOING OPTIMIZATION & MAINTENANCE

### Step 8.1: Monitor Student Progress
**Track:**
- Enrollment rate (how many sign up)
- Completion rate (how many finish)
- Engagement (video watch time, worksheet completion)
- NPS/satisfaction (how happy are they)
- Churn (who's dropping out and why)

**Tools:**
- Platform dashboard (built-in analytics)
- Surveys (at end of each module)
- Community Slack feedback
- Email open rates

### Step 8.2: Continuous Improvement
**Quarterly Review:**
- What's working? (highest completion, highest satisfaction)
- What's not? (highest dropout, lowest satisfaction)
- Student feedback themes (repeated requests)
- Competitor landscape (what else are they taking)
- Market changes (is the content still relevant)

**Make Updates:**
- Re-record sections that are confusing
- Update examples (keep fresh)
- Add new modules if students request depth
- Improve worksheets based on feedback
- Refresh marketing messaging based on what resonates

### Step 8.3: Scale to Multiple Cohorts
**Transition from Beta to Ongoing:**
- Decide on cohort schedule (monthly, quarterly, always-open)
- Standardize the module release schedule
- Automate emails for each cohort
- Create FAQ based on student questions
- Build peer community (forum, Slack)

## COMMON PITFALLS & HOW TO AVOID THEM

### Pitfall 1: Course Too Long
**Problem:** Instructor talks for 2+ hours per module, students don't finish
**Solution:** Target 30-45 min per module. If it's longer, split into 2 modules.

### Pitfall 2: No Clear Action Steps
**Problem:** Students watch video but don't know what to do next
**Solution:** Every module must have 3-5 concrete action steps. Template this.

### Pitfall 3: Worksheet Disconnected from Lesson
**Problem:** Video teaches X, but worksheet asks about Y
**Solution:** Write worksheet while editing video. Ensure direct alignment.

### Pitfall 4: Poor Transcription Readability
**Problem:** Descript transcription is word-for-word and hard to read
**Solution:** Always edit for readability. Remove filler, add structure, clarify.

### Pitfall 5: No Community
**Problem:** Students feel alone, isolated, less likely to complete
**Solution:** Create Slack channel or forum. Encourage peer support.

### Pitfall 6: Forgetting to Promote
**Problem:** Course exists but no one knows about it
**Solution:** Marketing plan = part of production timeline, not afterthought.

### Pitfall 7: Ignoring Student Feedback
**Problem:** Course stagnates because feedback isn't incorporated
**Solution:** Quarterly review + iteration cycle. Show students their feedback matters.
