## 4. Brand Voice & Tone

> Inherits from Launchpad brand guidelines with personal brand modifications noted below.

### Voice Descriptors (Personal Brand Layer)
- Conversational — talks like he does in voice-to-text messages, not like a marketing deck
- Specific — tool names, dollar amounts, real examples. Not "AI can help you" but "Claude wrote 30 captions in under 10 minutes"
- Generative — gives things away. No teasing. No gatekeeping.
- Confident but not cocky — "here's what worked for me" not "here's the only way"
- Real-time narrator — sharing what's being figured out as it's being figured out

### Tone Spectrum
| Context | Tone |
|---------|------|
| Building in Public content | Raw, conversational, first-person, present tense |
| LinkedIn / B2B | Slightly more composed, same directness, professional framing |
| Teaching content | Clear and plain — no jargon unless you explain it immediately |
| Origin story / personal | Warm, human, occasional humor |
| Entrepreneurship hot takes | Direct, opinionated, earned authority |

### Language to Use
- "I'm building..." / "We built..." / "Here's what happened when..."
- First-person throughout — this is a personal brand
- Tool names specifically (Claude, Obsidian, GHL, Notion, etc.)
- Numbers and specifics ("3 hours saved", "$297/month in tools")
- "No gatekeeping" framing
- Las Vegas references when organic
- "Vibe coding" — his own coined term for learning to code through Claude
- "The Brain" (referring to his Obsidian knowledge base)

### Language to Avoid
- "Guru" or any self-proclaimed expert language
- Generic AI hype ("AI is going to change everything")
- Corporate jargon ("leverage," "synergy," "disrupt")
- Over-produced, scripted delivery
- Anything that sounds like it was written by a marketing team
- "I can't believe I'm sharing this" — fake exclusivity
- Stats or claims about his own reach that aren't verified (see Kyle Fuller follower note)

### Benchmark Test (From Campaign Brief)
> Read it out loud. If Anthony would say it in a casual conversation with a business owner he just met, it passes. If it sounds like a press release, cut it.

## 6. Content Pillars

The personal brand runs 5 content pillars, structured under the "Building in Public" series. Content is primarily viral/discovery-focused on IG/TikTok with evergreen authority layers on LinkedIn/YouTube.

| Pillar | Type | What It Covers | CTA Mechanic |
|--------|------|----------------|-------------|
| **The Tool Drop** | Viral + Evergreen | A tool Anthony just watched, tried, or implemented — shared real time | "Comment BUILD / TOOLS — I'll DM you the list" |
| **The Build Log** | Evergreen (BTS) | What got built this week in the AI OS — specific, plain-language | Weekly newsletter → LP University |
| **The Fear Breakdown** | Viral (engagement) | Addressing a specific AI fear or myth blocking business owners | Comment triggers + newsletter |
| **The Win** | Evergreen (authority) | Concrete result with receipts — agent output, time saved, client deliverable | LP University CTA |
| **The Glossary** | Evergreen (educate) | Plain-language explainers of AI terms: agent, bot, prompt, workflow, MCP | Follow + newsletter |

### Content Type Breakdown (Monthly Target)
From Brain/Intelligence/Content Types and Formats.md — recommended personal brand mix:
- 5 vlogs / BTS / Build Logs
- 4 talking heads (educational or insights)
- 4 carousels (educational tips, AI breakdowns, founder stories)
- 4 trend/personality reels (The Fear Breakdown, engagement)
- 4 LinkedIn written posts (adapted from IG, B2B framing)
- **Total: ~21 posts/month (5/week cadence)**

### What "Good" Content Looks Like for This Brand
Every high-performing post in this series will:
1. Start with a specific hook that names a real thing (not "AI can help you")
2. Give away the actual information — no teasing
3. Have Anthony's face or voice attached to it (human layer on AI content)
4. Include a comment-to-DM trigger or newsletter CTA
5. Feel like it was made in the warehouse, not in a studio

## Onboarding Process Notes — For Internal Review

> This section is for Anthony's eyes and the onboarding build with Abril. This run was a live stress test of the Client Onboarding Agent (Agent #1). Notes on what worked, what was clunky, and what a real paying client onboarding would hit friction on.

### What Worked Well

**1. Internal source richness.** The Brain already had extraordinary context on Anthony — Teams/Anthony Mendoza.md, Intelligence/AI Systems — ChatGPT Extract.md, Business Strategy — ChatGPT Extract.md, Daily notes, AI campaign intel, the full Building in Public campaign brief. Sections 1-7 of the context note filled almost entirely from internal sources without needing external research. This is a direct payoff of the Brain investment.

**2. Cross-source signal synthesis.** The agent successfully triangulated voice patterns across: Slack DM captures (voice-to-text style), Claude Code memory entries (decision-making patterns), Brain daily notes (what gets built and why), and the campaign brief (tone guide). The result is a nuanced voice section that a Content Creator agent can actually work from — not "professional and friendly" but specific behavioral descriptors.

**3. The Notion search was fast and surfaced relevant pages.** The search for "Anthony Mendoza personal brand" returned 10 results including team pages, accountability meeting transcripts, and relevant context. The integration is working.

**4. Web research surfaced a critical finding fast.** The @anthony.launchpad Instagram handle EXISTS with 1,242 followers and 76 posts — and the current bio is misaligned with the Building in Public pivot. This is an actionable gap that would have been missed without the rep scan step. The podcast appearance was also confirmed from search. This step consistently earns its place in the workflow.

### What Was Clunky

**1. WebFetch was not permitted.** The online reputation scan step depends on being able to fetch actual page content (LinkedIn profile, Instagram profile HTML, website copy). Without WebFetch, the agent was limited to search result snippets. This means:
- LinkedIn follower count: not retrieved
- LinkedIn bio/about text: not retrieved (only job title from snippet)
- @launchpadbizdev Instagram post themes: not retrieved
- Website copy and messaging: not retrieved
- All marked as context gaps rather than filled sections

For a real paying client onboarding, WebFetch being unavailable would be a significant limitation. Abril's build session should confirm whether WebFetch will be available in the production onboarding flow or whether a human-step research pass is needed.

**2. The Client Context Template doesn't exist at the expected path.** The initialization sequence calls for reading `Brain/Resources/Client Context Template.md` — but the Brain uses a flat structure, not `Brain/` as a prefix. The actual path is `Resources/Client Context Template.md` within the vault. The template also wasn't found at that path — the Launchpad Business Development project note was used as the de facto template reference instead. The template path in the agent definition needs to be corrected, or the template itself needs to be created.

**3. Notion fetch failed on individual page IDs.** The notion-fetch tool requires a parameter named `id` but the MCP was called with a URL string. This meant individual Notion pages (Anthony's team profile, meeting notes) could not be fetched for additional context. The Notion search worked; the individual page fetch did not. This is a tooling issue to fix before Abril's build session.

**4. Brand inheritance creates ambiguity for downstream agents.** Saying "inherits Launchpad guidelines" works for now, but the Brand Compliance Agent will need a clear rule: "For personal brand content, use Launchpad brand guidelines EXCEPT for [specific overrides]." Without those overrides documented, brand compliance checks will either over-flag differences or under-flag them. The branding interview needs to happen before the Brand Compliance Agent can gate-check personal brand content with confidence.

**5. No Client Context Template to instantiate from.** The workflow says "create from template." There is no template file. The onboarding agent had to build structure from scratch using the 13-section standard from memory/system prompt. For Abril's build, either: (a) create the template file, or (b) document that the agent builds from the system prompt definition directly.

### What a Real Paying Client Onboarding Would Hit Friction On

1. **Drive scaffolding requires the client's Google Drive to exist first.** A new paying client won't have a pre-configured shared drive. The agent can scaffold folders, but someone (Amber or Abril) needs to create the initial shared drive and share it before the agent can act. This is a human pre-step that isn't yet documented in the onboarding flow.

2. **GHL Location ID must come from a human.** The agent correctly flags this as a required input (stored in Section 2 of this note as "not yet created"). For a paying client, this means: Amber creates the GHL sub-account → captures the Location ID → drops it in the onboarding Notion task → agent reads it from there. That handoff sequence needs to be documented and tested in Abril's session.

3. **Social handle verification requires WebFetch OR a human.** For a paying client, the online rep scan step is critical — it surfaces gaps between how a client talks internally vs. externally, finds existing content to reference, and catches any brand inconsistencies before content is created. Without WebFetch, a human research step is required. Options: (a) restore WebFetch permission in the production flow, (b) Abril does a 20-minute manual scan and drops URLs into the onboarding Notion form, (c) use Apify for Instagram scraping once OAuth is set up.

4. **The "approved examples" section will almost always be empty at onboarding.** No client fills this in upfront. The agent should be instructed to explicitly prompt the human for 2-3 reference posts during the onboarding call rather than leaving it blank. This is the highest-leverage piece of context for the Creative Designer and Content Creator agents — they cannot produce on-brand visual content without it.

5. **The branding interview needs to be in the Notion onboarding form, not just the Brain note.** The 25 questions above were written for Anthony to answer async in Slack. For paying clients, these should be pre-loaded into the Notion client portal as a structured form that Abril sends in the welcome sequence. The answers flow back into the Brain note automatically via the Notion-Brain sync. This is the Phase 1 build with Abril.
