## 2. Newsletter Structure

Each contributor section follows this format:

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Headline** | Bold, attention-grabbing title |
| **Hook** | Opening line or question that pulls the reader in |
| **Introduction** | Sets up the topic and why it matters |
| **Paragraphs 1-2** | Core content — the insight, breakdown, or lesson |
| **Closing** | Ties it together with a takeaway or call to action |

**Section specs:** 500-600 words per section, 2-3 pictures per section.

**No fixed recurring elements** (no standard CTA block, featured links section, or quote of the week — though these could be added).

## 4. Tone & Voice Analysis

### Anthony Mendoza — "The Philosopher"
- **Style:** Reflective, essay-driven, mentor-like. No bullet points — pure narrative.
- **Structure:** Uses mantra-style subheadings ("You Are What You Consume," "Create Your Reality," "Set Your Intention")
- **Tone:** Deep, intentional, challenges the reader without talking down. Reframes familiar ideas ("you are what you eat" → "you are what you consume").
- **Signature move:** Philosophical frame with a personal challenge at the close. Feels like a creative manifesto, not a marketing blog.
- **References:** Calls back to previous episodes/issues — builds continuity.
- **Example title:** "See Like an Artist, Think Like a Marketer"

### Kyle Fuller — "The Tactician"
- **Style:** Direct, practical, actionable. Uses numbered lists and bullet points.
- **Structure:** Problem → Why it matters → How to fix it → Quick tips to apply today
- **Tone:** Confident, no-nonsense, speaks from experience. Talks like a peer who's been in the trenches.
- **Signature move:** Breaks down a specific platform problem and gives 3-5 actionable steps. Ends with a punchy one-liner.
- **References:** Links to supporting video content (YouTube).
- **Example title:** "Out of State Leads? Here's What's Actually Happening"

## 5. Content Direction

### What Works
- Topics relatable to **any marketer** — not niche-specific
- Big brand campaigns broken down with small business takeaways
- New marketing trends and tools
- Advice tied to Content → Leads → Systems framework
- Platform-specific tactical advice (Meta ads, Instagram, TikTok, etc.)

### What Amber Wants More Of
- Trendy marketing campaigns from major brands (Nike, Gentle Monster, HelloFresh, etc.)
- Hot topics pulled from real campaigns
- New marketing trends and tools
- Advice on pulling leads through ads
- Content → Leads → Systems applications

### Content Pillars for Newsletter Topics
1. **Campaign Breakdowns** — What major brands are doing and what small businesses can steal
2. **Platform Updates** — Algorithm changes, new features, what's working now
3. **Tools & Tech** — New AI tools, marketing tools, free/affordable stacks
4. **Lead Generation** — Ad strategy, targeting, CRM, follow-up systems
5. **Creative & Mindset** — The art of creation, brand building, intentional consumption
6. **Content Strategy** — What to post, where, how often, and why

## 8. Answers to Key Questions

### How many sections per issue?
Each section is 500-600 words with 2-3 pictures. Number of sections per issue varies — can be single-author or multi-contributor. Structure within each section is consistent: Headline → Hook → Introduction → Paragraphs 1-2 → Closing.

### Who writes what?
Primary: Amber (ads/systems), Kyle (content/video tactics), Emigen (TBD), Cynthia (TBD). Occasional: Anthony (philosophy/creative), Abril (TBD). Each person writes about their area of expertise in their own voice.

### The different tones across contributors
- **Anthony:** Philosophical, reflective, essay-style, mentor energy
- **Kyle:** Tactical, direct, actionable, practitioner energy
- **Amber:** Systems/data-oriented (to be confirmed with examples)
- **Emigen & Cynthia:** To be confirmed

### How the newsletter flows from section to section
Hero branding → "LETS TAKE OFF..." → Individual contributor sections (self-contained, each with their own headline and structure) → Footer. Each section stands on its own — readers can engage with one or all.
