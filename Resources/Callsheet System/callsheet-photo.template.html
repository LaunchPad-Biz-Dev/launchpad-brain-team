Format guide

Every hero setup gets shot in more than one ratio. Frame loose enough to crop — decide the format at the camera, not in post.

Vertical 9:16
Reels · TikTok · Stories

Handheld POV walk-ups, action close-ups, single-subject portraits, product raised toward camera. Subject centered, headroom at the top for the Stories UI.

Feed 4:5 — primary
IG feed · 1080×1350

The default social format — most of the shoot composes for this. Populated space, team lineup, product in hand. Keep a dark band top or bottom for typography.

Square 1:1
Feed carousel · grid

Centered product beauty, macro detail, overhead flat-lay. Symmetry does the work — subject on the center axis, even margins.

Horizontal 16:9
Website header · PR · press

Ultra-wide space hero, establishing frames, group behind the counter or desk. Shoot wider than feels right — web headers crop hard top and bottom, and press re-crops again.

Behind the scenes
Client channels · our case study

Casual frames of the crew and the setup — lights going up, a shot being framed, the team working. Cheap to grab and clients use them.

Technical notes

Lighting and color

  • {{LIGHT_CONDITION}}
  • Shoot RAW. Set a custom white balance off a neutral surface, then re-check it in each distinct zone. Don't trust auto WB in mixed light.
  • Expose for the brand surface and let the surroundings fall off. Dark surround gives design room for type.

Flash

  • {{FLASH_DIRECTION}}
  • Flash solves mixed-temperature light on people. Use it on all portrait and lifestyle work unless the look forbids it.

Glass

  • 24–70 f/2.8 for coverage — space, context, group work.
  • Fast prime, 35 or 50 f/1.4, for lifestyle and low light.
  • Macro or 90mm for product detail.
  • Something longer for candid customer shots without invading space.

Framing for downstream use

  • Every hero gets a horizontal, a 4:5, and a 9:16 version.
  • Leave negative space. Design places text in the photo's natural dark zones. Edge-to-edge busy frames can't be used for graphics.
  • Volume target: {{FRAME_TARGET}} frames. Cover the Must-get blocks ruthlessly.

Do not shoot

  • ✕{{EXCLUSION_1}}
  • ✕No dirty surfaces, trash, clutter, or back-of-house mess in frame.
  • ✕No competitor branding legible in the background.
  • ✕No customers who decline or look uncomfortable. Move on immediately.

Clearances Confirm before the day

These are the things that kill an asset in post if they aren't handled on the day.

  • 1Access window. Can we be in early for clean plates before the space populates? This is almost always the highest-value ask.
  • 2Talent releases. Signed releases for every staff member. Bring customer releases too, or keep customer shots non-identifiable.
  • 3Third-party IP. Any venue, league, franchise, or partner marks in frame need written clearance — and organic social, paid, website and print are different permissions.
  • 4Restricted subject matter. {{RESTRICTED_NOTE}}
  • 5Shooting permissions. Where can we set up? Tripods and light stands allowed? Escort required?
  • 6On-site wrangler. Who walks with us to pull people and clear customers? Name and cell number.
  • 7Camera-ready staff. Who is cleared, and will they be in fresh uniform?
  • 8Clean product pulled from unused stock.
  • 9Deliverable date. When are first selects needed?
