Audio plan Un-fixable if missed

Capture

  • Lav on every speaking subject. Under clothing only if you can hear it rustle-free — otherwise clip it visible and let the editor handle it.
  • Boom as the backup and the character. Lav is safety, boom is quality. Record both to separate tracks.
  • Room tone, 60 seconds, every location. Absolute silence on set, camera rolling. This is the single most commonly missed item on a shoot and there is no way to recreate it.
  • Wild ambience — the space actually working. Crowd, machines, music bleed. Two minutes.

Monitor

  • Headphones on for every take. Not "check it after."
  • Watch levels — peak around −12 dB, never touch 0.
  • Listen for HVAC, fridges, and music. Ask for them to be turned off, and set a reminder to turn them back on.

Sync

  • Clap at the head of every take if timecode isn't running between bodies.
  • Note which takes are keepers as you go — the editor should not have to guess.

Format guide

Decide the delivery ratio at the camera. A 16:9 master cropped to 9:16 loses the frame the shot was built on.

Vertical 9:16
Reels · TikTok · Shorts

Dedicated passes, not crops. Subject centered with headroom for captions and UI. Keep the action in the middle third — the top and bottom get covered.

Horizontal 16:9
YouTube · website · presentation

The hero edit and all interview coverage. Shoot 4K so a 1080 vertical crop stays clean if it's needed later.

Square 1:1
Feed video · paid

Centered composition that survives both crops. Useful for product motion and any paid cutdown.

Technical notes

Camera settings

  • {{RESOLUTION}} · {{FRAME_RATE}} base · 180° shutter ({{SHUTTER}}).
  • Slow motion at 120fps for hero product moments only — it eats card space fast.
  • Picture profile: {{PICTURE_PROFILE}}. Same profile on every body or the grade won't match.
  • Variable ND on anything outdoors. Do not fix exposure with shutter speed.
  • White balance set manually and matched across bodies. Note the Kelvin value here: {{KELVIN}}.

Movement discipline

  • Start static, end static. Every moving shot needs a locked handle at both ends so the editor has a cut point.
  • One move per shot. A pan that becomes a tilt that becomes a push is unusable.
  • If the shot doesn't need movement, don't move. Static and well-composed beats drifting gimbal.

Cards and power

  • Format cards in-camera at the start. Never mid-day.
  • Offload to two destinations before anything is deleted. Verify the copy.
  • Battery count for the day: {{BATTERY_PLAN}}.

What the editor needs

  • Footage organized by block as you shoot — separate folders or cards per section.
  • A shot log noting keeper takes and problem takes.
  • This callsheet, checked off, handed over with the footage.

  • ✕{{EXCLUSION_1}}

  • ✕No customers who decline or look uncomfortable. Move on immediately.

Clearances Confirm before the day

These are the things that kill a deliverable in post if they aren't handled on the day.

  • 1Access window. Can we be in early to light and set A-roll before the space populates?
  • 2Talent releases. Signed releases for every person who speaks or is identifiable on camera. No release, no usable soundbite.
  • 3Music. Any music playing in the space is in your audio bed and is a copyright problem. Ask for it off during A-roll.
  • 4Third-party IP. Venue, league, franchise or partner marks need written clearance — organic, paid, website and broadcast are different permissions.
  • 5Power and rigging. Where can we plug in, set stands, run cable? Escort required?
  • 6On-site wrangler. Who pulls talent and clears background? Name and cell number.
  • 7Talent availability. Exactly when is each on-camera person free, and for how long?
  • 8Deliverable date and cut list. How many edits, what lengths, what ratios?
