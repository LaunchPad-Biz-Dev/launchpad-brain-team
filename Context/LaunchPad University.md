# LaunchPad University

## What It Is
LaunchPad University is a community and educational platform launched in 2026. It is a core part of Launchpad's growth strategy and brand authority engine. The platform houses video courses, transcribed training modules, downloadable worksheets, interactive planning tools, and a community space for peer learning.

Positioning: **A legitimate educational institution, not a "course bro" platform.** Built on the Hormozi legitimacy model: selective enrollment, focus on skills + network + status (not income promises), rigorous standards, data-driven success tracking, and community exclusivity.

---

## Strategic Purpose

**As a Sales/Marketing Tool:**
- **Top of funnel magnet** — Free and low-ticket content attracts engaged entrepreneurs
- **Pre-qualification filter** — Effort required in courses screens out tire-kickers; commitment leads to better clients
- **Trust building at scale** — Establishes Launchpad as authority beyond 1:1 services; teaches the "LaunchPad way"
- **Lead generation engine** — Engaged course takers naturally convert into sales conversations
- **Brand authority** — Positions LaunchPad as educators AND practitioners (we teach what we do)

**As an Internal Tool:**
- **Onboarding resource** — New hires learn Launchpad's methodology, culture, and evolution immediately
- **Reference documentation** — Team members access consistent processes and frameworks
- **Language alignment** — Everyone speaks the same framework (Better Content → Better Leads → Better Systems)
- **Execution guide** — Step-by-step playbook for delivering services to clients
- **Talent pipeline** — Top performers from LP University become potential team members

**As a Scalable Revenue Stream:**
- Tiered membership structure (free → low-ticket → certification → high-ticket inner circle)
- Monetizes knowledge without adding operational strain
- Creates community ecosystem that compounds over time

### **CHAPTER 1: STRATEGY & PLANNING**
**Foundation for everything. Build the quarterly plan first. Taught by Anthony Mendoza.**

*Philosophy: Strategy beats tactics every time. Plan the quarter before executing anything.*

#### **Module 1: Quarterly Planning Setup & Goal Alignment**
**What It's About:** Why quarterly planning beats monthly thinking. Strategy vs execution. Why most marketing fails before it starts.

**Key Concept:** A quarterly plan is not a calendar. It's a decision-making framework.

**Topics Covered:**
- Why planning quarterly creates focus and eliminates chaos
- The difference between business goals and marketing goals (they are not the same)
- Alignment across leadership, sales, and marketing
- Quarterly planning worksheet setup

**Action Steps:**
1. Define the quarter you are planning for
2. Write your primary business goal
3. Translate to 1-2 marketing goals
4. Confirm leadership alignment

**Worksheet Output:**
- Business name
- Quarter & year
- Business goal(s)
- Marketing goal(s)

**Key Concept:** Focus creates results. Trying to do everything achieves nothing.

**Topics Covered:**
- Four core marketing objectives: Awareness, Lead Generation, Conversion, Retention
- Identifying your current bottleneck
- Why choosing all four kills momentum
- Secondary objectives (optional, supporting role only)

**Action Steps:**

#### **Module 3: Audience First Thinking (Who Are We Actually Talking To?)**
**What It's About:** Defining your ideal customer with emotional and demographic depth. Goes beyond demographics into emotional state and real problems.

**Key Concepts:**
- Your customer is the hero. Your brand is the guide. (StoryBrand framework)
- Surface-level problems vs deep emotional problems
- Specificity creates clarity. Clarity creates sales.
- Your audience determines how you speak, what you say, and where you show up

**Topics Covered:**
- Going deeper than surface-level problems
- Real problems reveal emotional context and friction
- Primary audience definition (demographics + lifestyle + values)
- Emotional state mapping (before/after transformation)
- Brand archetype selection (Hero, Sage, Creator, Mentor, Magician, etc.)
- Where your audience spends time and what they consume

**Examples Covered:**
- Real estate: From "people who want to buy homes" to "first-time buyers who've had bad experiences" or "luxury buyers seeking discretion"
- Fitness: From "people who want to be healthy" to "busy professionals tired of unrealistic gym culture"

**Action Steps:**
1. Identify 3-5 real problems your audience faces (not surface-level)
2. Define primary audience with demographics, lifestyle, values
3. Identify emotional state before and after working with you
4. Complete brand archetype quiz
5. Write how archetype influences your tone

**Worksheet Output:**
- Primary audience description
- Core problems (3-5)
- Emotional state before / after
- Archetype(s) selected
- Tone direction based on archetype

#### **Module 4: Messaging Strategy (What Is Your Marketing Actually About This Quarter?)**
**What It's About:** Creating a single, focused message statement that aligns with goals and resonates with audience. Supporting angles that reinforce, not replace, the main message.

**Key Concept:** Repetition is not boring. Inconsistent messaging is confusing. One clear sentence + supporting angles = authority.

**Topics Covered:**
- The core messaging statement (fill-in-the-blank framework)
- How messaging must align with business goals + marketing goals + audience
- Supporting angles that build depth without changing direction
- Emotional + logical + credibility angles
- Why consistency builds authority

**Examples Covered:**
- Weak: "This quarter our marketing is about selling more homes"
- Strong: "This quarter our marketing is about luxury real estate solutions for busy business owners who want a streamlined, discretion-focused buying experience"
- LaunchPad Example: "This quarter our marketing is about LaunchPad University — educating business owners on how to build structured marketing systems that create consistent growth"

**Supporting Angle Examples:**
For product launch: Results (testimonials) + Benefits (how it improves performance) + Proof (data/comparisons)

For luxury real estate: Streamlined process + Discretion/privacy + Time efficiency

**Action Steps:**
1. Write the core messaging statement: "This quarter, our marketing is about __"
2. Confirm alignment with business + marketing goals
3. Identify 2-3 supporting angles that reinforce (not replace) the message
4. Ensure each angle can produce multiple content pieces

**Worksheet Output:**
- Core messaging statement
- Supporting angle #1 (with description)
- Supporting angle #2 (with description)
- Supporting angle #3 (optional)

### **CHAPTER 2: CONTENT CREATION**
**Execute the strategy. Production workflow from research to posting. Taught by [[Kyle Fuller]] (Content Creator, @vcy on TikTok/Instagram, 2.5M+ followers on TikTok)**

*Philosophy: Content is not random. It follows patterns. You can learn the patterns, reverse-engineer high performers, and create consistently good content.*

#### **Module 1: Pre-Production — Research (Intentional vs Mindless Scrolling)**
**What It's About:** How to research content intentionally. Reverse-engineering psychology. Pattern recognition. Why trends are transferable, not invented.

**Key Concept:** Don't just watch content. Dissect it. Understand why it stopped the scroll. Understanding the "why" lets you adapt it.

**Topics Covered:**
- What to study in high-performing content: hooks, transitions, camera angles, CTAs, pacing
- How to reverse-engineer psychology: Why did this stop the scroll?
- Pattern recognition: What structures repeat in high-performing content?
- Adaptation framework: Trends are transferable (you adapt, not invent)
- Research worksheet: Breaking down a high-performer

**Actionable Framework:**
For any high-performing video, identify:
- **Hook:** First 2-3 seconds. Why did you stop scrolling?
- **Structure:** Setup, conflict, resolution. What's the narrative arc?
- **Camera work:** Static? Moving? Close-ups? Wide shots?
- **Transitions:** How do scenes change? Fast cuts? Fades?
- **Text/Captions:** Where do they appear? How long? What do they reinforce?
- **Audio:** Music, voiceover, natural sound? How does it pace?
- **CTA:** What does the creator ask you to do at the end?
- **Pacing:** How fast does it cut? When does it slow down?

**Action Steps:**
1. Pick 5 high-performing videos in your niche
2. Break down each using the framework above
3. Look for patterns across all 5
4. Write down 3 trends you notice
5. Brainstorm how to adapt one trend to your message

**Worksheet Output:**
- Research breakdown of 5 high-performers
- Patterns identified
- Adaptation ideas for your core message

#### **Module 2: Pre-Production — Planning (Shot Lists, Briefs, Roles, Timeline)**
**What It's About:** Turning research and your core message into an executable plan. What to shoot, how to shoot it, who's involved, and when.

**Key Concept:** Bad planning = chaos on set. Good planning = efficient shooting, confident execution, better creative output.

**Topics Covered:**
- Translating your message into content concepts
- Shot list creation (what specific shots do you need?)
- Content brief development (what's the idea, why does it matter, what's the emotional goal?)
- Role assignment (who films, who's talent, who directs, who manages sound?)
- Timeline and scheduling (how many hours, what's the schedule, when do we break?)
- Location scouting (where are we shooting, what permits needed, what's the vibe?)

**Shot List Example:**
For a testimonial video about results:
- Wide shot of client in natural setting
- Close-up of client speaking directly to camera
- B-roll of them using the product/service
- Detail shot of results (before/after comparison, metric, etc.)
- Close-up of their reaction/emotion
- End frame with call-to-action

**Action Steps:**
1. Write your content concept (1 sentence)
2. Define the emotional goal (what should viewers feel?)
3. Create a shot list (all shots needed)
4. Write the content brief (what's the idea, why, what's the payoff?)
5. Assign roles to your team
6. Create a shooting timeline

**Worksheet Output:**
- Content concept
- Emotional goal
- Complete shot list
- Content brief
- Team roles assigned
- Shooting timeline

#### **Module 3: Production — Filming (Minimal Equipment, Intentional Execution)**
**What It's About:** Actually capturing video. Minimalist mindset, technical basics, safety, professionalism.

**Key Concept:** You don't need a $5K camera to create great content. You need intentionality, good light, good sound, and multiple takes.

**Topics Covered:**
- Minimal equipment mindset (phone cameras are sufficient, what matters is light and sound)
- Essential equipment for quality: good lighting, microphone, tripod/stabilizer
- Setting the scene: establishing shots that provide context
- Capturing detail shots: close-ups, specific angles
- Getting multiple takes and angles (safety net approach)
- Audio best practices (clean sound matters more than perfect video)
- On-set professionalism and safety
- Directing on set (how to give direction, get the best takes, keep energy up)

**Technical Breakdown:**
- Lighting: 3-point lighting (key, fill, back), natural light utilization
- Camera: Phone (iPhone 12+) works; rig it with stabilizer
- Audio: External mic (lav or shotgun), test levels, backup audio
- Staging: Clean background, remove distractions, establish depth

**Action Steps:**
1. Scout location before shoot day
2. Set up lighting (test before talent arrives)
3. Test audio (multiple takes, check levels)
4. Film establishing shots first (get coverage)
5. Film talent/main content (get 3+ takes of each shot)
6. Capture B-roll and details
7. Shoot closing/CTA frame
8. Backup all files (redundancy)

**Worksheet Output:**
- Location notes and setup plan
- Equipment checklist
- Lighting diagram (if needed)
- Audio test results
- Shot count completed
- File backup confirmation

#### **Module 4: Post-Production — Editing (Hook-First, Pacing, Consistency)**
**What It's About:** Turning raw footage into finished content. Organization, software, hook-first editing, pacing, text, color, CTA placement.

**Key Concept:** Editing is where content gets its power. The first 2-3 seconds determine if viewers stay. Everything after must maintain momentum.

**Topics Covered:**
- File organization and naming (so you can find clips in 6 months)
- Editing software basics: CapCut (primary for social), Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve
- Hook-first editing (strongest hook in first 2-3 seconds)
- Pacing and rhythm (when to cut fast, when to hold, how to use silence)
- Text overlays and captions (hierarchy, timing, readability)
- Color grading and visual consistency (color grade all clips to same grade)
- CTA placement (where do you ask for action?)
- Export settings (format and quality for each platform)

**Editing Framework:**
1. **Hook (0-3 seconds):** Strongest visual or sound. Why should they keep watching?
2. **Story (3-30 sec):** Setup problem, introduce character, build curiosity
3. **Peak (Peak moment):** Emotional turning point or big reveal
4. **Resolution/CTA (Last 5-10 sec):** Close the loop, ask for action

**Text Best Practices:**
- All important information in captions (viewers watch muted)
- Hierarchy: Size, color, timing
- Avoid cluttering (text should support, not distract)
- Use for emphasis, not everything

**Color Grading:**
- Create a "look" (consistent color palette across all content)
- Use LUTs (color lookup tables) for speed and consistency
- Grade before effects
- Export in highest quality available

**Action Steps:**
1. Organize all files systematically before editing
2. Create rough cut (get the story right first)
3. Add hooks to the opening (strongest moment first)
4. Cut for pacing (remove silence, tighten transitions)
5. Add text and captions (all dialogue + key points)
6. Color grade all clips to consistent look
7. Add music and sound effects (don't overdo it)
8. Place CTA clearly at end
9. Export for each platform (different aspect ratios/settings)
10. Backup final files

**Worksheet Output:**
- File organization system
- Editing checklist
- Color grade reference (screenshot of applied LUT)
- Export settings per platform
- Backup confirmation

#### **Module 5: Platform Optimization (TikTok, Reels, YouTube, LinkedIn, Stories)**
**What It's About:** Taking your finished video and optimizing it for each platform's unique requirements and algorithms.

**Key Concept:** The same video doesn't work the same way on every platform. Aspect ratio, length, audience type, and algorithm all matter.

**Topics Covered:**
- TikTok/Instagram Reels optimization (fast, punchy, hooks, sound-on)
- YouTube optimization (longer form, SEO, thumbnails, pattern interrupts)
- Facebook/LinkedIn optimization (conversational, captions, slower pacing)
- Story formats and considerations (ephemeral, different aspect ratio)
- Hashtag strategy per platform
- Posting times and frequency
- Algorithm considerations per platform

**Platform Specifics:**

**TikTok/Instagram Reels:**
- Aspect ratio: 9:16 (vertical)
- Length: 15-60 seconds (sweet spot 15-30)
- Sound: Critical (trending audio + good quality)
- Hook: First 1 second is everything
- Captions: ALL dialogue, key points on screen
- Pacing: Fast cuts, pattern interrupts every 2-3 seconds
- CTA: Hook for follow/share, not always "buy"

**YouTube:**
- Aspect ratio: 16:9 (landscape)
- Length: 8+ minutes (algorithm favors longer watch time)
- Thumbnail: Custom thumbnail critical, high contrast, face/emotion
- SEO: Title, description, tags matter
- Captions: Full, searchable transcription
- Pattern interrupts: Every 15-30 seconds (b-roll, text, visual change)
- CTA: End screen for subscribe, playlist, next video
- Cards: Link to related content, form

**Facebook/LinkedIn:**
- Aspect ratio: 1:1 or 4:5 (square or vertical)
- Length: 2-3 minutes optimal (longer form than Reels)
- Sound: Many watch muted, captions essential
- Pacing: Slower, more conversational
- Captions: Text reads naturally, colloquial
- CTA: Link in comments, website link in description
- Engagement: Ask questions, replies matter for algorithm

**Action Steps:**
1. Define platforms where your audience lives
2. Resize video for each platform (maintain quality)
3. Create platform-specific captions/text
4. Write platform-specific descriptions
5. Create custom thumbnail (YouTube)
6. Research trending audio/hashtags
7. Plan posting schedule (consistent, optimal times)
8. Set up scheduling tool (Meta Business Suite, Later, Buffer)

**Worksheet Output:**
- Video exports for each platform
- Platform-specific descriptions/captions
- Hashtag research
- Posting calendar (30-day minimum)
- Performance tracking template

## Course Creation & Asset Format Standards

### Lesson Format (Standardized Template)
Every lesson follows this structure:

1. **Overview** — What this lesson is about, why it matters
2. **Core Concept** — The big idea (1-2 paragraphs)
3. **Breakdown** — Detailed explanation with examples
4. **Action Steps** — Concrete things to do before moving forward
5. **Worksheet Guidance** — Which fields on the worksheet to complete
6. **Key Takeaways** — 3-5 bullet points summarizing the lesson

### Asset Formats

#### Video Assets
- **Chapter intros** — 5-10 min overview of what's ahead
- **Module videos** — 15-45 min deep dives (taught by instructors)
- **Live recordings** — Q&A or case study walkthroughs
- **Screen recordings** — Software tutorials (Notion, GoHighLevel, editing software)
- **Case studies** — Real client examples with permission and results

#### Supporting Materials
- **Full transcripts** — Cleaned, readable, searchable text version of every video
- **Worksheets** — PDF + Notion templates for planning/execution
- **Checklists** — Step-by-step action items for each module
- **Scripts** — Email, SMS, sales, content idea scripts
- **Resource lists** — Tools, software, inspiration sources, templates
- **One-pagers** — 1-page visual summaries of key concepts

#### Interactive Elements
- **Notion integration** — Editable templates linked from courses
- **Downloadable assets** — Design templates, email swipes, ad copy swipes
- **Quizzes** — Low-stakes, learning-focused assessment (not gatekeeping)
- **Community** — Slack channel or Circle for course takers to discuss + support each other

### Production Workflow (Descript-Based)

1. **Record** — Instructor records video (raw, doesn't need to be perfect)
2. **Transcribe** — Descript automatically transcribes video
3. **Edit transcription** — Clean up, remove filler, add structure, clarify pacing
4. **Use as curriculum** — Polished transcription becomes the written lesson
5. **Edit video** — Create edited video version (remove dead air, add text overlays)
6. **Create assets** — Break transcription into worksheets, one-pagers, scripts
7. **Upload** — Video + transcription + supporting materials to platform

This workflow ensures every video has a complete written component for accessibility and SEO.
