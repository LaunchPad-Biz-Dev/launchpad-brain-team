# Content Types and Formats

## Overview
This document catalogs every type of content that Launchpad creates across its clients and internal work. It serves as a reference for AI agents, creative teams, and clients to understand content production capabilities, use cases, and best practices.

**Purpose:** Understand what content exists, when to use it, and what makes it effective.

---

## VIDEO CONTENT TYPES

### 1. **Talking Heads / Direct-to-Camera**

**What it is:**
A person speaking directly to the camera, typically used for education, testimonials, CTAs, or authority building.

**Typical Length:**
- Short-form (TikTok/Reels): 15–60 seconds
- Medium (YouTube): 1–3 minutes
- Long-form (YouTube, Podcasts): 5+ minutes

**Best Practices:**
- Clear lighting on face
- Neutral or branded background
- Natural delivery (not overly scripted)
- Eye contact with camera
- High-quality audio (lavalier or Rode mic)

**When to Use:**
- Education/FAQ content
- Personal brand building
- Testimonials
- CTA-driven campaigns
- Authority positioning

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- BME tech answering HVAC FAQs ("How to make your AC last longer")
- Ben Nadig lawyer breaking down personal injury myths
- Upbeat Health founder explaining primary care approach

**Typical Revisions/Issues:**
- Stiff delivery (coach for natural cadence)
- Poor lighting (fix in pre-production)
- Audio inconsistencies (use wireless mics consistently)

---

### 2. **Testimonials / Social Proof Videos**

**What it is:**
A customer or user speaking about their experience with a service/product. Often conversational and authentic-feeling.

**Typical Length:**
- 30–90 seconds (most effective)
- Can be shorter (15–30 sec) for social media

**Best Practices:**
- Real customer, real story
- Allow natural pauses and emotion
- Ask open-ended questions to get natural responses
- Shoot in customer's location (home, workplace, etc.) when possible
- Capture reactions and genuine moments

**When to Use:**
- Building trust with cold audiences
- Conversion-focused campaigns
- Long-form content (YouTube)
- Reputation building

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- Blue Mountain Energy: Customer talks about choosing BME over expensive competitors
- Ben Nadig Law: Client shares how case was resolved
- HVAC service: Homeowner explains how service solved problem and saved money

**UGC (User-Generated Content) Style:**
- Scripts provided, but talent delivers naturally
- Often shot on iPhone for authentic feel
- Used extensively in paid ads due to trustworthiness

---

### 3. **B-Roll + Process Videos**

**What it is:**
Footage of work in progress, tools being used, environments, or processes happening in real-time. Usually paired with voiceover or graphics.

**Typical Length:**
- 5–30 seconds (as insert in other videos)
- 1–3 minutes (standalone educational)

**Best Practices:**
- Capture wide, medium, and close-up shots
- Get hands doing work (detail shots)
- Show before/after when applicable
- Use gimbal for smooth movement
- Shoot from multiple angles

**When to Use:**
- Educational content (showing HOW)
- Documentary/behind-the-scenes content
- Building credibility (showing real work)
- Process explanations

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- HVAC: Technician replacing air filter, spraying AC unit, checking thermostat
- Medical: Healthcare provider performing wound care, setting up equipment
- Production: Behind-the-scenes of photo/video shoot

---

### 4. **Skits / Comedy Content**

**What it is:**
Entertaining, scripted (or semi-scripted) video content with comedic timing, often following a trend or meme format. Goal is to stop the scroll and entertain.

**Typical Length:**
- 15–45 seconds (TikTok/Reels optimized)

**Best Practices:**
- Strong hook in first 2–3 seconds
- Clear punchline or reveal
- Multiple takes for different deliveries
- Match trending audio/format
- Fast-paced editing

**When to Use:**
- Building personality and brand awareness
- Reaching younger audiences
- Increasing engagement and shares
- Making educational content more memorable

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- Taco Bell Cantina: "Dollar Bill Trap" skit (Haley grabs Kyle's hand when reaching for dollar, pitches Cantina features)
- BME: "Deadly Weapon or Not" skit (swiping left/right on household objects, all could be weapons)
- BME: "My HVAC boyfriend" skit (showing off tools, flirty trend-based)

**Trend-Based Formats:**
- "POV: You..." (point-of-view setup)
- "_____ Trend" (spinning, pointing, flinching, etc.)
- Challenge-based (rock paper scissors, mukbang race, etc.)
- Reaction skits (to absurd situations or clips)

---

### 5. **Educational Series / How-To Content**

**What it is:**
Sequential videos teaching a skill, answering a question, or breaking down a complex topic into digestible pieces.

**Typical Length:**
- 2–4 minutes per episode
- Can be split into shorts (30–60 sec) for social

**Best Practices:**
- Clear structure (problem → solution → CTA)
- Use graphics to highlight key points
- Conversational tone (not lecturing)
- Practical, actionable tips
- Series consistency (same host, similar visual style)

**When to Use:**
- Building authority in niche
- Lead generation through education
- YouTube channel building
- Evergreen content strategy

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- BME: "How to make your AC last longer" series (15 different FAQs)
- Ben Nadig: "Myth Busting" personal injury law series
- Upbeat Health: "What primary care really is" vs. urgent care

---

### 6. **Reaction / Commentary Videos**

**What it is:**
Creator watches and responds to other content (clips, trends, competitor behavior) with legal/expert commentary or humor.

**Typical Length:**
- 1–3 minutes (TikTok/YouTube Shorts)
- Can be longer for YouTube

**Best Practices:**
- Show original clip + reaction side-by-side
- Provide actual value/insight (not just laughing)
- Match energy level of content
- Use zoom/pause moments for effect
- Clear call to action after reaction

**When to Use:**
- Positioning as expert/authority
- Legal/medical commentary
- Trend-jacking (react to viral content)
- Building personality

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- Ben Nadig: Lawyer reacts to accident videos, explains liability
- BME: Reacting to competitor pricing or exaggerated claims
- Medical: Reacting to health misinformation (myth-busting)

---

### 7. **Interview / Conversation Videos**

**What it is:**
Two people having a conversation (Q&A format) typically covering expertise, experience, or insights.

**Typical Length:**
- 10–30 minutes (YouTube, podcasts, webinars)
- 2–5 minutes (clips extracted for social media)

**Best Practices:**
- Both people facing camera naturally (not side-by-side staring)
- Clear audio from both participants
- Interesting questions that prompt good stories
- Extract highlight clips for social
- B-roll cutaways to break up talking heads

**When to Use:**
- Podcast episodes
- Guest collaborations
- Deep expertise dives
- Personal brand building
- Thought leadership

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- Anthony + Partner interviews (business discussions)
- Ben Nadig + Client interviews (case discussions)
- Upbeat Health + Patient interviews

---

### 8. **Behind-the-Scenes / Day-in-the-Life**

**What it is:**
Raw, often unscripted footage of daily work, team culture, or process. Humanizes brand and builds connection.

**Typical Length:**
- 30–90 seconds (social media)
- 3–5 minutes (YouTube vlog-style)

**Best Practices:**
- Authentic, unpolished (don't over-edit)
- Show real moments (laughs, struggles, wins)
- Diverse perspectives (multiple team members)
- Sound design matters (natural audio + light music)
- Storytelling arc (beginning, middle, end)

**When to Use:**
- Building team culture and trust
- Recruiting (showing workplace)
- Personal brand expansion
- Content consistency between campaigns

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- Launchpad office vlog (Anthony filming team, decisions, meetings)
- Launchpad Productions: Behind-the-scenes of shoot day
- Client services: "A day in the life of a technician"

---

### 9. **Unboxing / Product Showcase**

**What it is:**
Showing a physical product, unpacking it, or demonstrating its features in a visually appealing way.

**Typical Length:**
- 30–90 seconds

**Best Practices:**
- Close-up detail shots
- Show product from multiple angles
- Highlight unique features
- Use ASMR or satisfying sounds
- Lifestyle context (person using it)

**When to Use:**
- E-commerce product launches
- BrandWithUs print goods promotion
- Product-focused businesses

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- BrandWithUs: Unboxing branded merchandise
- Merchandise shoot: Showing off printed products

---

### 10. **Animation / Explainer Videos**

**What it is:**
Motion graphics, animated text, or animated characters explaining a concept or process.

**Typical Length:**
- 1–3 minutes
- Can be shorter (15–30 sec) for social

**Best Practices:**
- Clear visual metaphors
- Consistent style throughout
- Good pacing (not too slow, not too fast)
- Clear narration or voiceover
- Text/captions for accessibility

**When to Use:**
- Explaining complex processes
- Product/service overviews
- Educational content
- Abstract concepts

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- Explainer: How wound care process works (Upbeat Health)
- Explainer: How personal injury claim process works (Ben Nadig)
- Animated logo or intro for channel

---

### 11. **Livestream / Real-Time Video**

**What it is:**
Live, unedited broadcast of an event, Q&A, launch, or activity.

**Typical Length:**
- 15 minutes – Several hours
- Clips extracted for social after

**Best Practices:**
- Test audio/video before going live
- Have moderation/engagement plan
- Multiple camera angles if possible
- Clear call to action (purchase, sign up, follow)
- Archive and repurpose highlights

**When to Use:**
- Product launches
- Educational webinars
- Live events or workshops
- Q&A sessions
- Real-time engagement with audience

## PHOTO/GRAPHIC CONTENT TYPES

### 1. **Hero / Brand Photography**

**What it is:**
High-quality, professionally shot photos showcasing the brand, product, or team in their best light. Often used as main imagery on website or campaigns.

**Best Practices:**
- Professional lighting setup
- Branded backgrounds or meaningful locations
- Consistent style with rest of brand
- Vertical and horizontal variations
- High resolution (300 dpi for print, high quality for web)

**When to Use:**
- Website homepage
- Campaign headers
- Social media profile images
- Print collateral
- Ad creative

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- BME branded truck (multiple angles)
- Upbeat Health facilities (warm, welcoming)
- Ben Nadig office/courtroom setting
- Team portraits and group photos

---

### 2. **Product/Service Close-Ups**

**What it is:**
Detail shots of products, tools, or services being delivered. Often used to show quality, craftsmanship, or care.

**Best Practices:**
- Macro/close-up lens for detail
- Clean, organized composition
- Natural or key lighting
- Show texture and quality
- Multiple angles/variations

**When to Use:**
- E-commerce product listings
- Social media carousel detail slides
- Educational content (showing tools, processes)
- Quality/trust positioning

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- HVAC: Close-ups of tools, filters, coils, gauges
- Wound care: Close-ups of bandages, instruments, technique
- BrandWithUs: Close-ups of printed product finishes, textures

---

### 3. **Lifestyle Photography**

**What it is:**
Photos showing products or services in real-world contexts (people using them, happy customers, real homes/businesses).

**Best Practices:**
- Natural, conversational poses (not stiff)
- Real environments (not overly staged)
- Show emotions (smiling, comfortable, confident)
- Diverse representation in people
- Action and interaction (not just standing)

**When to Use:**
- Social media content
- Website testimonial sections
- Campaigns showing outcomes
- Trust-building content

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- HVAC: Homeowner comfortable in cool home, tech talking to customer
- Upbeat Health: Patients feeling cared for, family moments
- BrandWithUs: Customers using/wearing branded products

---

### 4. **Social Media Carousels**

**What it is:**
Multi-slide Instagram posts (3–10 slides) telling a story, providing tips, or educating step-by-step. Highly sharable and engagement-driving.

**Slide Breakdown:**
- Slide 1: Hook (main topic/question)
- Slides 2–(N-1): Tips, points, or story progression
- Last slide: CTA or summary

**Best Practices:**
- Consistent visual style across slides
- Large, readable text
- Use brand colors
- One key point per slide
- Text + graphics (not just walls of text)

**When to Use:**
- Educational tips
- Myth-busting
- How-to breakdowns
- Listicles (5 tips, 10 reasons, etc.)
- Testimonial stories

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- Upbeat Health: "What makes wound care effective" (5 slides)
- BME: "Why chronic wounds take longer" (carousel)
- Ben Nadig: "Types of personal injury cases" (carousel)

---

### 5. **Single-Image Social Posts**

**What it is:**
A standalone image designed for Instagram, Facebook, or other social platforms with text overlay/caption.

**Best Practices:**
- Strong visual hierarchy
- Clear, bold text
- Compelling hook
- Brand-aligned colors
- Readable at mobile size

**When to Use:**
- Offers and CTAs
- Quotes or wisdom
- Event promotion
- Quick tips
- Visual storytelling

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- "$29 AC Tune-Up" static graphic
- "Care With Dignity" message from Upbeat Health
- "You only get one shot at this" CTA from Ben Nadig Law

---

### 6. **Moodboards / Design Direction**

**What it is:**
Collection of reference images, color palettes, typography samples, and style examples to guide design and creative direction.

**Best Practices:**
- Curated inspiration (not random)
- Include color swatches with hex codes
- Show typography pairings
- Include do/don't examples
- Written descriptions of each element

**When to Use:**
- Briefing designers
- Client alignment on aesthetic
- Brand guideline creation
- New campaign launches

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- Upbeat Health moodboard: Warm, inclusive, uplifting healthcare aesthetic
- BME moodboard: Professional but approachable HVAC brand
- Client brand guidelines update

---

### 7. **Flat Lay / Styled Composition**

**What it is:**
Artfully arranged products, tools, or props photographed from above (flat lay) or at an angle. Often used to show variety or quality.

**Best Practices:**
- Uncluttered, intentional arrangement
- Consistent lighting (usually bright, even)
- Use of negative space
- Color coordination or contrast
- Props that tell a story

**When to Use:**
- Product showcases
- "Here's what we use" content
- Tools/gear displays
- Social media aesthetic posts

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- HVAC tools laid out (wrench, gauge, filter, etc.)
- Upbeat Health: Medical supplies arranged professionally
- BrandWithUs: Printed products arranged to show range

---

### 8. **Infographics / Data Visualization**

**What it is:**
Visual representation of data, statistics, or processes using charts, icons, and text.

**Best Practices:**
- Accurate data representation
- Clear labeling
- Consistent color scheme
- Icons that support message
- Readable at mobile size

**When to Use:**
- Educational content (showing statistics)
- Process breakdowns
- Comparison content
- Reports or research findings

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- "Why AC fails faster" stats infographic (percentage breakdown)
- "Types of personal injury cases" breakdown (visual taxonomy)
- Upbeat Health: Healing progress timeline visualization

---

### 9. **Ad Creative / Static Ads**

**What it is:**
Optimized graphics designed specifically for paid advertising on Meta, Google, or other platforms. Constrained by aspect ratio, text limits, and mobile readability.

**Best Practices:**
- Keep text to <20% of image (Meta requirement)
- Large, readable headline
- Clear CTA
- High contrast colors
- Test multiple versions

**When to Use:**
- Facebook/Instagram ads
- Google Display ads
- TikTok ads
- YouTube thumbnail-style ads

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- "$29 Tune-Up" ad graphic (Facebook)
- "Free Estimate" ad (Google Display)
- "Feel Better With Intention" IV therapy ad (Upbeat Health)

---

### 10. **Print Materials**

**What it is:**
Designs meant for physical printing: flyers, postcards, business cards, posters, brochures, etc.

**Best Practices:**
- 300 dpi resolution
- CMYK color mode
- Bleed lines for edge-to-edge printing
- Font embedding for text
- High contrast for readability

**When to Use:**
- Local business promotion
- Event marketing
- Direct mail campaigns
- In-store displays

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- BrandWithUs: Branded flyers, business cards, postcards
- BME: Print flyer promoting seasonal offers
- Upbeat Health: Clinic brochure

---

### 11. **Email Graphics / Headers**

**What it is:**
Visual assets designed for email marketing (headers, featured images, footer graphics, etc.).

**Best Practices:**
- Optimized for email clients (some don't render well)
- Alt text for accessibility
- Mobile-responsive sizing
- Clear hierarchy
- Brand-consistent

**When to Use:**
- Email marketing campaigns
- Newsletter headers
- Promotional emails
- Welcome sequences

---

### 12. **Web Graphics**

**What it is:**
Optimized graphics for websites: banners, section backgrounds, call-to-action buttons, etc.

**Best Practices:**
- Web-optimized file sizes
- Responsive design (works at all screen sizes)
- Fast load times
- Accessible (alt text, high contrast)
- Consistent with website brand

**When to Use:**
- Website design
- Landing pages
- Blog header images
- CTA buttons and graphics

## AUDIO CONTENT TYPES

### 1. **Podcast Episodes**

**What it is:**
Long-form audio content (typically 20–60 minutes) featuring interviews, storytelling, or educational content.

**Best Practices:**
- Clear audio quality (professional microphone)
- Consistent episode structure
- Engaging host/guest dynamic
- Intro/outro music and bumpers
- Chapters or segments for easy navigation
- Show notes and transcripts

**When to Use:**
- Building authority and thought leadership
- Long-form audience engagement
- Repurposing into clips for social
- Building email list through listeners

**Examples from Launchpad:**
- Launchpad podcast: Anthony interviewing business owners, sharing insights
- Ben Nadig Law: Legal trends and case discussions
- Upbeat Health: Healthcare provider conversations

---

### 2. **Voiceover / Narration**

**What it is:**
Professional narration recorded to accompany video content. Can be B-roll narration, educational explanation, or brand voice.

**Best Practices:**
- Professional voice talent (or client if brand voice)
- Clear, well-paced delivery
- Proper pronunciation
- Good microphone and sound design
- Scripts timed to video edits

**When to Use:**
- Educational/explainer videos
- B-roll narration
- Commercial/ad voiceover
- Audiobook or long-form content narration

---

### 3. **Music / Soundtracks**

**What it is:**
Background music used in videos or audio content. Can be licensed, royalty-free, or original.

**Best Practices:**
- Matches tone and pacing of content
- Licensed properly (royalty-free or paid)
- Appropriate volume level (doesn't drown out other audio)
- Consistent style across brand

**When to Use:**
- Video background tracks
- Podcast intros/outros
- Social media content
- Branded audio experiences

## WRITTEN CONTENT TYPES

### 1. **Social Media Captions**

**What it is:**
Text accompanying a photo, video, or carousel post on social media. Can be casual, educational, or sales-focused.

**Best Practices:**
- Hook in first line
- Conversational tone
- Scannable (use line breaks)
- Include relevant hashtags
- Clear CTA

**When to Use:**
- Every social media post
- Directing viewers to links/actions
- Telling story behind visual

---

### 2. **Scripts / Talking Head Copy**

**What it is:**
Written scripts for people to deliver on camera. Can be word-for-word or bullet-point outline.

**Best Practices:**
- Natural, conversational language (not stiff)
- Shorter sentences (easier to deliver)
- Bullet points often better than full scripts
- Mark emphasis/pacing notes
- Test readability

**When to Use:**
- Talking head filming
- Educational content
- CTA recordings
- Interview preparation

---

### 3. **Blog Articles / Long-Form**

**What it is:**
1,000–3,000+ word written content optimized for search engines and reader engagement.

**Best Practices:**
- SEO-optimized headlines and structure
- Clear sections with subheadings
- Conversational tone
- Include visuals
- Links to related content
- Call-to-action at end

**When to Use:**
- SEO strategy
- Thought leadership
- Educational authority building
- Email lead magnets

---

### 4. **Email Copy**

**What it is:**
Written content for email marketing (subject lines, body copy, CTAs).

**Best Practices:**
- Compelling subject line (high open rate)
- Scannable body (short paragraphs)
- Clear value proposition
- Single strong CTA
- Mobile-optimized

---

### 5. **Copywriting for Ads**

**What it is:**
Persuasive text designed to sell, inform, or drive action in paid advertising.

**Best Practices:**
- Attention-grabbing headline
- Benefit-focused (not feature-focused)
- Conversational tone
- Clear CTA
- A/B testable

## CONTENT MIX & STRATEGY

### Typical Content Calendar (Monthly Mix)

**For Social Media-Focused Brand (like Launchpad Personal Brand):**
- 5 vlogs (behind-the-scenes, daily moments)
- 4 talking heads (educational or insights)
- 4 carousels (educational tips or founder stories)
- 4 fun/trend reels (personality-driven)
- 4 empathetic/vulnerability posts (real leadership moments)
- Total: 21 posts/month (5 per week)

**For Service-Based B2B (like BME or Ben Nadig):**
- 8 educational talking heads (FAQ/myth-busting)
- 8 testimonial/social proof videos
- 8 educational carousels
- 4 offer graphics (rotating)
- 2 case study/deep-dives
- Total: 30 assets/month

**For Upbeat Health (Mix of Education + Brand):**
- 12 educational carousels (wound care focus)
- 12 static graphics (primary care, brand)
- 8 short educational videos
- 4 testimonials/case studies
- 2 interview/expert content
- Total: 38 assets/month

## WHAT MAKES CONTENT "WORK"

### High-Performing Content Patterns at Launchpad

**Educational Content Works When:**
- Solves a real problem (customer pain point)
- Conversational tone (not corporate)
- Specific and actionable (not vague advice)
- Shows expertise without being preachy
- Includes clear CTA (what to do next)

**Entertainment Content Works When:**
- Hook is in first 2–3 seconds
- Unexpected twist or punchline
- Relatable to audience
- Follows trending format (but with brand twist)
- Shareable (people want to send to friends)

**Sales/Conversion Content Works When:**
- Clear value proposition upfront
- Addresses specific objection
- Social proof included (testimonial, stat, logo)
- Friction-reducing CTA (easy next step)
- Urgency without being pushy

**Brand/Culture Content Works When:**
- Shows real people and moments (not overly polished)
- Demonstrates values in action
- Emotional connection (not just corporate)
- Authentic voice (not generic brand speak)
- Makes audience feel part of community
