# A Mi Manera Coffee — Content Framework

**Status:** draft, built 2026-10-06 from the public site plus Anthony's direction. Not yet reviewed with the founders. Vocabulary corrected 2026-10-07: the five below are pillars (not buckets); Awareness/Desire/Story/Choice are the buckets. Promote to Projects/ once approved.

**Client:** A Mi Manera Coffee Company, specialty coffee trailer, 11416 N FM 620, Austin TX 78726. Wed–Sun until 3pm, closed Tuesdays. Founders: Steven (co-founder; Monterey Park / Inland Empire; sales and biz dev, "the midfielder") and Leilani (co-founder; San Bernardino County, raised military kid in El Paso; first job barista at Rally Point; flavor and menu, "heart and soul"). A tío and sobrina. IG/TikTok @amimaneracoffee.

**Positioning (their words):** "Luxury, raised in the streets. High-end espresso. Lowrider soul." Mission: "elevate and proudly display Chicano culture." "Luxury belongs to everyone. Especially the people who were told it didn't." "Not for everyone. Solo para ti." Four principles: Train the skill · The Anti-Rush · Pour it up · A nuestra manera. Influences: Estevan Oriol, Mister Cartoon, José Guadalupe Posada, Cypress Hill. Trailer: candy paint, gold leaf, Sacred Heart.

**Menu:** Honeycomb Cold Brew $6.50 (cold brew, salted honey) · Horchata Matcha Latte $8 (ceremonial matcha over family horchata) · Cafecito: americano, cappuccino, cold brew, cortado, espresso, latte · Aguas frescas: jamaica + seasonal · Flavors: Café de Olla, Orange Vanilla, Prickly Pear, Salted Honey, seasonal · Milks: whole, oat, half & half, horchata · One size 16 oz, tax included. Revenue lines: daily trailer traffic, event bookings/catering, Block Party (Summer 2026, text signup).

## The insight: the trailer is the brand
Strong identity, no visible system for repeatable content. The product is the drink and the object (trailer as hero). Craft is the proof of "luxury." Culture is the moat. Chicano first: show the Mexican-American experience with pride in its full range (El Paso, Inland Empire, Austin), not just the LA lowrider chapter.

## Buckets (the four, as in Taco Bell)

| Bucket | Purpose for A Mi Manera | Main jobs |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Trailer and signature drinks recognizable without a logo | Inspire, Entertain |
| Desire | Make the senses want the drink now (carried mostly by The Craft pillar) | Entertain, Inspire |
| Story | Build the world: people, culture, craft, community | Empathize, Prove |
| Choice | Easy to show up, order, or book | Convert, Educate |

Short local funnel: most of the volume in Desire and Story.

## Chicano pride, not costume: authenticity guardrails
1. The founders are the source. Not their lived experience or a lineage they can name → doesn't post.
2. Chicano is wide, not one aesthetic. LA lowrider/cholo is one chapter. Rotate chapters.
3. No props: no bandanas, Dickies, hairnets, fake tattoos, "cholo" captions on people who don't live it.
4. Spanish as it's spoken. Spanglish the way Steven and Leilani talk. Never translated for effect or italicized as exotic.
5. Credit the lineage by name. Pay collaborators.
6. Sacred imagery is faith, not a flex. Never on a joke, a sale or a meme.
7. Celebrate, don't explain. No "did you know" captions for outsiders. Community first.
8. History carries weight: specifics and respect, or not at all.
9. No stereotypes for laughs. No "spicy" copy, no sombrero energy, no Cinco bar-promo tone.
10. Austin, not LA cosplay. Texas Chicano is its own thing.
11. Luxury language stays: "high-end," "ceremonial-grade," "the anti-rush."
12. The two-nod test: would a Chicano elder and a Chicano teenager both nod?
