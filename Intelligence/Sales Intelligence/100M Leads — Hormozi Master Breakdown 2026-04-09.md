## Executive Summary

`$100M Leads` is Hormozi's second book and the direct sequel to `$100M Offers`. Where `$100M Offers` answered *"what should I sell?"* (a Grand Slam Offer), `$100M Leads` answers *"who do I sell it to?"* — specifically, how do I get strangers to show interest in the thing I'm selling. That interest is what Hormozi calls an **engaged lead**, and engaged leads are the true output of advertising. Not leads. Not list sizes. Engaged leads.

The single most important idea in the book is this: **advertising always works — the only question is how efficiently.** If your ads don't perform, it's not because the channel is broken, saturated, or rigged against you. It's because you're not doing enough volume, or not doing it well enough, or not doing it in enough places. Every other insight in the 67,000 words flows from that premise. Hormozi's job is to show you how to do more, do it better, and do it in new places — reliably.

Structurally the book hinges on the **Core Four**: the only four ways a single person can let another person know about anything. You can contact people one-to-one (private) or one-to-many (public). You can contact people who already know you (warm) or people who don't (cold). That gives you: Warm Outreach, Posting Content, Cold Outreach, and Paid Ads. Each has its own chapter, its own 6–10 step playbook, its own benchmarks, and its own failure modes. Once a single operator has maxed out the Core Four, the book pivots to **lead getters** — other people who do the Core Four on your behalf: customers (referrals), employees, agencies, and affiliates.

Why this matters for Launchpad specifically: Anthony stated verbatim that Launchpad has a lead problem and wants to 10x the business with an AI sales team. Hormozi's book is the most direct, most practical, most system-minded answer to that problem in the market. It's written for owner-operators. It's written for people who are not "natural" salespeople. It's written for businesses that need to stop hoping and start doing deterministic advertising math. Launchpad is all three. We already have the infrastructure (GHL, Notion, the Brain, the 9-agent marketing team) to automate large chunks of it. We have Anthony's personal brand (@anthony.launchpad, 2.5M+ Kyle follower audience upstream of Launchpad). We have clients that could be lead getters. What we don't have yet is a systematized, measurable machine — and this book gives us the vocabulary, the phases, the benchmarks, and the frameworks to build one.

One important caveat before we go deeper: Hormozi's own businesses are gyms, supplement brands, software, and info-products. He is not an agency operator. Several of his recommendations need translation to the service agency model (see "What Needs Adaptation" below). But the core frameworks — Value Equation, Core Four, Lead Magnets, Content Unit, LTGP:CAC, Referral Program structure, Affiliate Launch method — transfer cleanly. This note extracts them in Hormozi's language so any future Launchpad sales agent can operate on shared ground truth.

## Core Frameworks

### The Lead Definitions (and why they matter)

- **A lead** = a person you can contact. Email address, phone number, knock on a door. That's it.
- **An engaged lead** = a lead who has *shown interest* in what you sell. They clicked, opted in, replied, raised a hand. This is what we're actually buying when we run ads.
- **The business growth equation:** only two ways to grow a business — get more customers, or make them worth more. `$100M Leads` is 100% focused on the first lever, broken into *more leads, better leads, cheaper leads, reliably (from lots of places)*.

Key principles: engaged leads are the output; leads are the input. Common mistake: optimizing for list size instead of engagement rate. Example from the book: Hormozi spent 8 weeks and $450 on his first webinar campaign and got 80 leads and 0 sales — he was celebrating the wrong number. When he swapped the webinar for a 13-minute case study, Leila's calendar filled overnight. Same lead count, totally different engagement, because the lead magnet was better.

### The Value Equation (load-bearing across the whole book)

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
         ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
                (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

This is actually from `$100M Offers` but Hormozi invokes it on roughly every other page of `$100M Leads`. Every ad, every script, every lead magnet, every landing page should visibly push against all four levers. Maximize dream + likelihood. Minimize time + effort. Common mistake: only addressing the top half (selling the dream) and ignoring the bottom half (hidden costs, effort, time). Hormozi: "If you struggle to give your stuff away for free, it means either people don't want it, they don't believe you, or the hidden costs are too high. Your 'free' stuff is too expensive."

### Lead Magnets (Section II)

Definition: a **complete solution to a narrow problem**. Free or very low cost. When it's consumed, it reveals another problem that your core offer solves. The pretzel-and-beer analogy: salty pretzels solve hunger, then reveal thirst, which the drink solves for money.

**The 7 Steps to an Effective Lead Magnet:**
1. Pick the narrow problem and who it's for. The next problem it reveals must be solvable by your core offer.
2. Pick the solution type: (a) Reveal a Problem (diagnosis, audit, speed test, termite inspection), (b) Sample or Trial (free adjustment, free ad run, Costco samples), (c) One Step of a Multi-Step Process (this book, free first coat of sealant, free financial calculators).
3. Pick the delivery: Software, Information, Services, or Physical Product. Each of the 3 solution types crosses with each of the 4 deliveries, giving up to 12 lead magnet shapes per narrow problem.
4. Test the name. Per Ogilvy, the headline is 80% of your advertising dollar. Hormozi tested the title of this book itself across multiple rounds to arrive at "$100M Leads — How to get strangers to want to buy your stuff."
5. Make it easy to consume — multiple formats, multiple devices. `$100M Offers` splits nearly 25/25/25/25 across ebook/physical/audio/video.
6. Make it darn good. Give away the secrets, sell the implementation. A sucky lead magnet doesn't protect your paid offer, it destroys your reputation.
7. Tell them what to do next (CTA) + give a reason to do it now. Reasons: scarcity, urgency, or "any reason at all" (the Harvard line-cutting study: any "because…" beats no reason).

Common mistakes: (a) advertising your core offer for a high-ticket service when a lead magnet would triple engagement at the same spend; (b) making the lead magnet "okay" instead of "paid-level"; (c) no CTA or a clever CTA instead of a clear one.

Example: Hormozi had nine-to-one calls with a business that offered a "game planning session" (code for sales call) as their cold outreach magnet. Take rates were meh. Swapping it for actually giving away free work 3x'd take rates and turned cold outreach into a monster channel for them.

### The Core Four (Section III)

The only four ways a single person can advertise. Two axes: audience type × communication mode.

|  | Warm (they know you) | Cold (strangers) |
|---|---|---|
| **1-to-1** | #1 Warm Outreach | #3 Cold Outreach |
| **1-to-many** | #2 Post Free Content | #4 Paid Ads |

#### #1 Warm Outreach

The cheapest, most reliable, most underused channel. Everyone has a warm audience even if they don't know it — phone contacts, emails, social followers, old customers. Hormozi built his first business, his gyms, and Gym Launch off warm outreach.

**The 10 steps:**
1. Get your list. Combine every platform: phone, email, each social, old CRMs. Most people find 1000+ contacts on their first pass.
2. Pick the platform you have the most contacts on.
3. Personalize the greeting using 1–3 facts you can find.
4. Reach out to 100/day, up to 3 attempts.
5. Warm them up with the **ACA framework: Acknowledge → Compliment → Ask.** Not a script, a shape of a conversation.
6. Make them an offer — but by asking "do you know anyone who…" instead of asking them to buy. This protects the relationship.
7. Make the first 5 customers free. You'll suck at first, and people forgive sucking far more when it's free. And free customers convert, refer, and give testimonials.
8. When you finish your first platform, move to the second-most-contacts platform, then the third.
9. Start charging once referrals come in. Raise the price every 5 sales.
10. Keep your list warm with ongoing value, and probe it with Dean Jackson's 9-word email: *"Are you still looking to [4-word desire]?"* One sentence, no frills. Deadly effective.

**Benchmarks:** 100 warm reach outs → ~20 replies → ~4 take free offer → ~1 becomes paying. Hormozi's money math: 500 warm reach outs/week × a $400 offer × 52 weeks = $104K/year floor, from nothing else.

Common mistake: thinking you "don't have a list." Everyone has a list.

#### #2 Post Free Content

Core insight: **the content isn't the compounding asset. The audience is.** Hormozi spent years not posting because he thought it was a waste. Saw Kylie Jenner, Huda Kattan, McGregor's Proper 12, and The Rock's Teremana build billion-dollar exits on audience and realized he'd been wrong. Spent $120K for 4 calls with a big influencer. The entire lesson: "You just gotta do more, bro." 10x the output → 10x the growth.

**The Content Unit = Hook → Retain → Reward.** The smallest possible piece that does all three.

- **Hook** — give them a reason to notice, via (a) topic (Far Past, Recent Past, Present, Trending, Manufactured — prefer personal experience), (b) headline (include ≥2 of Recency, Relevancy, Celebrity, Proximity, Conflict, Unusual, Ongoing), (c) format that matches the platform's best content.
- **Retain** — embed questions via Lists ("7 ways…"), Steps ("3 steps to…"), or Stories (every chapter of this book opens with one).
- **Reward** — satisfy the promise. "No such thing as too long, only too boring."

**The give:ask ratio.** Growing platforms over-give and under-ask. Mature platforms (TV, Facebook) run ~3.5:1. Hormozi's strategy: **give until they ask.** People get uncomfortable receiving without giving back, and they'll come to you. You grow the warm audience faster and you attract the best type of customers — ones who feel they're already getting the better end.

**Monetization modes:**
- **Integrated asks** — CTA inside content as long as the ratio stays high.
- **Intermittent asks** — every 10 or 11 posts, make an ask post.
- The "ask" is either your core offer or your lead magnet. Don't overthink it.

**Scaling:**
- **Depth then width** — master one platform, then add another. Advantage: compounding. Disadvantage: platform risk.
- **Width then depth** — get on all platforms early, then maximize. Advantage: reach + repurposing efficiency. Disadvantage: quality drops.
- **Rule of 100:** 100 primary actions per day for 100 days.

**7 lessons Hormozi learned making content** (every one is directly applicable to Anthony):
1. Switch from "How to" → "How I." Experience is bulletproof. Opinions aren't.
2. We need to be reminded more than we need to be taught. Repeat yourself more than feels comfortable.
3. Puddles → Ponds → Lakes → Oceans. Narrow first. Become king of a puddle.
4. Content creates tools for salespeople. Your best posts become assets reps drop into sales conversations.
5. Free content retains paying customers. Customers who consume your free stuff stay longer.
6. People don't have shorter attention spans; they have higher standards.
7. Avoid pre-scheduling posts. Manual posts outperform because of the close feedback loop.

**The discovery that shifted Hormozi's whole model:** in January 2020 his paid ads stopped working. The team surveyed existing gym owner clients — 78% had consumed at least TWO long-form content pieces before booking a call. He'd been giving paid ads all the credit. Free content was nurturing demand. "Free content gets you better returns on all advertising methods, even if it's hard to measure."

#### #3 Cold Outreach

The more advanced cousin of warm outreach. Three problems that warm doesn't have: no way to contact, strangers ignore you, strangers don't trust you. Solved in three steps:

1. **Build the list.** Software scrapers first, then brokers, then elbow grease in communities. Manual lists are freshest because nobody else has contacted those people.
2. **Personalize + Big Fast Value.** One to three pieces of personal info plus a lead magnet so valuable it's "stuff they actually pay for." Don't mediocre-lead-magnet your way to a reply. "Give away the farm."
3. **Volume.** Automate delivery (pre-recorded voice memos, templated emails). Automate distribution (dialers, blast tools). Follow up multiple times, multiple ways. Restart the list every 3–6 months because circumstances change.

**Benchmarks:** phone: 100 calls → 20% pickup → 25% take → 4 engaged leads/day. Email: shoot for 3% engaged lead rate (30% open × 10% reply). DM with personalized video: can hit 20% reply.

**Hormozi's cold outreach origin story:** COVID-19 killed his paid ads. A rejected job applicant messaged him on Instagram. Turned out that applicant's current company was doing $10M/month entirely on cold outreach — with 30 people. Hormozi partnered with the applicant. Month 1: zero sales. Month 2: $32K. Month 6: $320K. Today: millions per month. Took almost a year to scale. Lesson: **cold outreach takes 6–12 months. Set proper expectations.**

**7 benefits of cold outreach:**
1. No ad creative treadmill.
2. Competition can't see what you're doing.
3. Reliable — input determines output, so reverse-engineer from sales goal.
4. Fewer platform changes than paid ads.
5. Compliance is easier than paid ad platforms.
6. No spokesperson dependency → sellable business.
7. Hard to copy because it's all invisible infrastructure.

#### #4 Paid Ads

The fastest way to scale. Trade money for reach. Four problems, four solutions:

1. **Where.** Pick a platform where you're a consumer, you can target, you know the format, and you can afford minimum spend.
2. **Who sees it.** Target with lookalike audiences (upload your lead list — customers, warm, cold — to generate them) plus filters.
3. **The ad.** Callout + Value + CTA.
   - **Callouts** can be verbal (Labels — "Clark County Moms", Yes-Questions, If-Then Statements, Ridiculous Results) or nonverbal (Contrast, Likeness — "quack like a duck", Scene). Hormozi's advertising got 20x better when he focused effort on the first 5 seconds of every ad.
   - **Value — the What-Who-When framework.** **What:** 8 key elements (Dream/Nightmare, Likelihood/Risk, Speed/Delay, Ease/Effort) — show each lever and its opposite. **Who:** your customer plus the people giving them status (spouse, kids, rivals, bosses). **When:** past/present/future timeline. More angles = more interested viewers. Long ads just have more angles than short ads; both have the same callouts and CTAs.
   - **CTA** spelled out. Short domains, obvious buttons, easy phone numbers. Robert Cialdini's consistency principle: if you remind them of what they just did and link it to the next action, more people take the next step.
4. **Get permission to contact them.** Simple landing page, matched to the ad so the experience feels continuous.

**The 3 phases of scaling spend:** Track Money → Lose Money → Print Money.
- **Track Money.** Never spend a dollar without tracking infrastructure set up first.
- **Lose Money.** Budget 2x the 30-day cash you get from a customer to test a new ad. Expect to lose 9 out of 10 times; when one wins, 10x-100x down on it. "Paid advertising is a lot like a casino. With enough skill, you eventually become the house."
- **Print Money.** Once profitable, reverse your ad budget from your customer goal, not your cash balance.

**The efficiency formula: LTGP : CAC.**
- **LTGP** = Lifetime Gross Profit (not LTV — LTGP nets out fulfillment cost).
- **CAC** = Cost to Acquire Customer.
- **3 : 1 is the minimum viable ratio** Hormozi has seen. Below that, businesses stall.
- Diagnostic: if your CAC is *below* 3x industry average, the problem is your business model (LTGP). Focus there. If CAC is *above* 3x industry average, fix advertising.

**Client Financed Acquisition.** If your new customer pays more than (CAC + 30-day fulfillment cost) within the first 30 days, you can scale indefinitely on a credit card — because any business can get a 30-day float. The mechanism: aggressive upsells on the first purchase. Hormozi's entire scale strategy across seven years + five companies used this one trick.

**Three personal lessons from paid ads:**
1. Don't confuse sales problems with advertising problems (a company wasted 12 weeks and $150K thinking their ads were broken — ads worked; sales was the bottleneck).
2. Your best free content is often your best paid ad. UGC especially.
3. If you say you suck at tech, you'll suck at tech. Hormozi wasted 4 years saying he wasn't technical; fixed his site in 4 hours.

### Core Four On Steroids: More / Better / New

Once the Core Four are running, you get more leads three ways:
1. **More.** Rule of 100. You are almost certainly doing 1/100 the volume required to actually win. Hormozi's favorite framing: "What's stopping them from doing ten times what they're currently doing?"
2. **Better.** Test one thing per week per platform. Find the **constraint** — the step with the biggest drop-off — and fix it. Small improvements at the constraint yield disproportionate gains.
3. **New.** New placements → new platforms → new core four activity (in that order).

The **Size of the Pie Fallacy:** small-business owners assume their tiny slice of one platform is the entire market. Hormozi's example: a chiropractor client claimed a $15.1B industry was "saturated" after spending $30K/month on one platform. Another chiropractor in the same niche was spending $30K/*week* across four platforms. The market is always 10x–1000x bigger than you think.

### The 4 Lead Getters (Section IV)

You don't "do" lead getters. You use the Core Four to get them, and then *they* do the Core Four on your behalf. This is pure leverage.

#### Customer Referrals

Referrals are the cheapest, highest-LTGP, most exponential channel. If your referral percentage per month is greater than your churn percentage, the business compounds. This is how Dropbox and PayPal scaled.

**Why most businesses don't get referrals:** (1) their product isn't as good as they think; (2) they don't ask.

**6 ways to give more value (and earn referrals)** — maps directly to the 6 components of an ad:
1. **Call Outs → Sell better customers.** Find your lowest-churn, highest-LTGP customers and sell more of them. A portfolio company discovered 15% of their customers had all the characteristics; shifting to target only that segment broke a multi-year plateau.
2. **Dream Outcome → Set better expectations.** Lower your promises until close rates drop; stop just above that. This maximizes overdelivery.
3. **Likelihood → Get more people better results.** Find what the best customers did and force everyone to do it. Gym Launch found gyms that ran paid ads in the first 7 days tripled their LTGP; they made that a required onboarding step.
4. **Time Delay → Faster wins.** Break deliverables into smaller, more frequent pieces. BAMFAM (Book A Meeting From A Meeting). Deliver early, never late.
5. **Effort & Sacrifice → Keep making the product better.** Monthly loop: survey, find most common problem, fix, test with struggling customers, roll out, repeat.
6. **CTA → Tell them what to buy next.** Never let a happy customer wander. Front-end obsession + back-end neglect = referrals dying on the vine.

**7 ways to ask for referrals:**
1. One-sided incentive (pay entire CAC to referrer OR to friend).
2. Two-sided incentive (split CAC — the Dropbox/PayPal model).
3. Ask right at the point of sale ("who else would you want to do this with?").
4. Use referrals as a negotiation chip ("I'll cut the price if you intro 3 friends").
5. Referral events ("Bring a Friend" promotions).
6. Ongoing programs woven into every channel.
7. Unlockable bonuses (VIP status, extra service hours).

Hormozi's "One Question to Rule Them All": imagine the gods of advertising ban you from the Core Four and threaten to destroy your business forever if you violate the rule. You have one customer left. How would you treat them? Do that now.

#### Employees

The most valuable chapter in the book in Hormozi's own words. Why? Because you get *rich* from what you *make*. You get *wealthy* from what you *own*. A business that depends on you is worth almost nothing. A business that runs without you is worth 5–10x its annual profit. Employees bridge that gap.

**The Internal Core Four** — you use the same four methods to recruit employees that you use to get customers:
- Warm outreach → asking your network
- Cold outreach → recruiting
- Post content → job postings
- Paid ads → promoted jobs
- (Plus parallel lead getters: employee referrals, staffing agencies, trade associations.)

**Training framework: 3Ds.** Document → Demonstrate → Duplicate. Write the checklist, do it in front of them, have them do it in front of you. Rule: if they get confused, *we* got it wrong, not them.

**Diagnosing lead-getting employees:** if qualified leads aren't buying → sales problem. If leads aren't qualified → ad problem. Don't fire the wrong person.

Hormozi's payroll math: Acquisition.com gets 30,000 engaged leads/month from content. Team costs ~$100K/month. = $3.33 per engaged lead. Wildly profitable.

Personal mindset shift: stop thinking "nobody can do it like I do it." Everyone is replaceable — including you. Replace yourself as soon as you can so you can go build the next thing.

#### Agencies

Hormozi's unconventional take: **use agencies to learn a new method or new platform, not as a permanent service.** Traditional agency model is broken — they move their best people to their newest client the moment you sign. Instead, Hormozi's template: *"I want to do what you do. I'll work with you for 6 months, pay extra for you to explain your decisions, then I transition to consulting as I train my team."*

He once paid a guy $750/hour for 8 hours — $6,000 total — to sit in on calls and have the mechanics of Facebook ads explained. That $6K turned into millions. He now hires a "good enough" agency to get a new platform running, then a more elite agency at 4x the price to teach the advanced mechanics.

10 criteria for picking a good agency: word-of-mouth reputation, prominent clients, waitlist, clear process, long-term strategy, clear scope of what they need from you, regular meeting cadence, transparent reporting, good offer per the value equation, expensive (but not all expensive agencies are good).

#### Affiliates

The highest-leverage lead getter and the one that made Hormozi the most money. Definition: an independent business with its own warm audience that agrees to advertise your stuff for commission or free product.

**Why you want an affiliate army:** each affiliate brings another stream of leads. One super-affiliate signed to ALAN added 10 agencies/month, who added 50 local businesses/month, who added 2,500 leads/month. Three super-affiliates scaled ALAN from zero to $1.7M/month in 6 months.

**The 6-step build:**
1. **Find ideal affiliates** — answer "who's got my leads?" Categories: software, products, equipment, services, groups, events.
2. **Make them an offer.** The offer is a new revenue stream, not a product. Call out + value elements + CTA.
3. **Qualify them.** Make them a customer, make them an expert (paid certification), or both. Cost = 10–20% of what an average affiliate makes in year one. Paid affiliates pay attention.
4. **Figure out payout.** Pay a percentage of max allowable CAC. **3-tier structure:** Tier 1 (25% CAC) on sign-up; Tier 2 (50%) on activation; Tier 3 (100%) on sustained performance. Blended payout ends up below your max CAC, which improves your LTGP:CAC ratio.
5. **Get them advertising — Launch using Whisper → Tease → Shout.**
   - **Whisper** phase (weeks to years out): curiosity, behind the scenes, no specifics.
   - **Tease** phase (2 weeks out): reveal the product and the value elements.
   - **Shout** phase (last 3 days): CTAs, scarcity, urgency, every few hours.
6. **Keep them advertising — Integrate.** Three integration models: (a) affiliate gives your lead magnet away free with every sale of theirs; (b) affiliate sells your lead magnet (keeps cash, you upsell the core offer); (c) affiliate sells your core offer directly with revenue split.

Case studies: the tax prep business that does zero paid ads and runs entirely on affiliate launches + integration. Prestige Labs selling supplements through gyms at a $0.50+ payout per bottle. Chiropractors running free posture workshops through gyms, then integrating adjustments into new gym memberships.

## What Needs Adaptation

Hormozi's frameworks are excellent but his business context is not Launchpad's. Translation notes:

1. **He sells info products, gyms, supplements, and software.** Those have (a) low fulfillment cost, (b) scalable delivery, (c) recurring revenue natively. Launchpad sells services with significant human delivery cost and longer sales cycles. Translation: Launchpad's LTGP:CAC thresholds will look different. The 3:1 minimum still holds but the time to prove it will be longer because services have multi-month onboarding.

2. **"Give until they ask" is scary for a service agency.** Hormozi's advice to "give away the farm" as a lead magnet works when your marginal cost to fulfill is near zero. For Launchpad Productions especially, a free half-day shoot is a real cost. The adaptation: productize the giveaway (e.g. a "content strategy session" instead of actual production) and reserve the expensive giveaways for high-LTGP prospects only.

3. **The "first 5 customers free" rule.** Great for brand-new businesses. Launchpad is 10+ years in and has a reputation. Adaptation: apply this rule to *new service lines* Launchpad introduces, not the whole agency. For example, if we launch a "Building in Public" service, the first 5 clients get it free in exchange for case studies.

4. **Affiliates demand a structured product.** Hormozi's affiliate machine worked because supplements and software are easy to stock and fulfill. For Launchpad, affiliate partners will bring deals that still need to be qualified, sold, and staffed. Adaptation: affiliates bring leads to a lead magnet, not to the core offer. ## Agent Reflection — Honest Notes For Anthony

What stood out, what I disagree with, the highest-leverage move, and whether the read was worth the time.

**What stood out most:**

The book is structurally brilliant because every chapter builds into the next. Value Equation → Lead Magnet → Core Four → More/Better/New → Lead Getters is a single staircase. If Launchpad executes the first two steps well (Value Equation thinking baked into every deliverable + a killer lead magnet for the agency itself), the rest compounds. The agency has been skipping the foundation and trying to jump straight to "more channels."

The second thing that stood out is how much Hormozi hammers on **volume**. He's not selling tactics, he's selling a mental model: you're almost always doing 1/100th of the required work. The 300-flyers vs 5000-flyers story is the whole book in microcosm. Launchpad's AI sales team doesn't need to be clever — it needs to be ruthless about volume, because volume is what Anthony physically cannot produce but AI can.

The third: Hormozi is surprisingly aligned with how Launchpad already runs its own AI OS. The Internal Core Four, the Document-Demonstrate-Duplicate training framework, the "replace yourself" mindset — these are all already baked into how Anthony thinks about building Apollo and the marketing agent roster. The book lowers the activation energy to apply those same principles to sales.

**What I disagree with (or want to soften):**

1. *"Give away the farm" is risky advice for service businesses.* Hormozi built his fortune on goods that scale. Services don't scale the same way. The agency can follow the *intent* (over-deliver in the lead magnet) without following the *letter* (give away a real production shoot for free to every cold prospect). Launchpad must productize its giveaways.

2. *The 6–12 months cold outreach timeline is real but frustrating.* Hormozi glosses over how painful the first 3 months are. If Launchpad starts cold outreach and evaluates at month 2, it'll quit. The timeline has to be built into the plan and protected.

3. *The book is silent on sales conversations themselves.* This is intentional — it's the next book. But it means Launchpad's Sales Conversation Coach Agent is operating without a Hormozi framework for the actual close. We'll need to layer in complementary frameworks (SPIN, Chris Voss, or Anthony's own distilled style) until the $100M Sales book drops.

4. *Hormozi's employee math assumes low-skill frontline workers.* Launchpad has senior creatives, not frontline SDRs. The Internal Core Four still works, but "hire anyone with a good work ethic" doesn't apply to the existing Launchpad team. It applies more to *new hires specifically for sales ops.*

**The single highest-leverage move for Launchpad, specifically:**

Build one Grand-Slam lead magnet for Launchpad Business Development — a Hormozi-quality free offer that gives away so much value that the people who consume it feel obligated to pay — and put it at the center of `@anthony.launchpad`'s content and Anthony's warm outreach. That single asset unblocks every other channel. Without it, warm outreach has nothing to offer, content has nothing to convert with, cold outreach has nothing to scale, and paid ads have nothing to send people to. With it, every channel becomes a distribution mechanism for the same engine.

My specific recommendation: the **"Free Launchpad Marketing Audit"** — a 30-minute human-delivered audit where a Launchpad team member walks a prospective client through their current content, ads, and funnel, gives away every insight, and proposes a path forward. This is the direct analogue of Hormozi's case-study lead magnet from the book's opening story — "I sold 19 people at $299 each" after filming himself walking through a gym's ad account for 13 minutes. Launchpad already does this work for every pitch. The only change is to productize it, give it a name, and advertise it as the lead magnet instead of the core offer. Low fulfillment cost, massive trust build, trains the AI Sales Team on real prospect data, and converts at a much higher rate than cold-pitching services.

**Top 3 highest-leverage moves before building any sales agents:**

1. **Anthony answers the "Open Questions for the Sales Session" document** (`sales/context/sales-session-open-questions.md`). Every proposed agent in the roster assumes answers Anthony hasn't given yet: LTGP per service, max allowable CAC, which Core Four channel to lead with, which avatar to chase first, and what the first lead magnet is. Anthony is not going to quit. The job of the AI sales team is to roll the die more times per day than any human possibly could. That's the whole game.
