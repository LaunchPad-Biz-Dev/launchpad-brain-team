## Executive Summary

`$100M Leads` answers *who do I sell to*. This one answers *what should I sell, and how do I present it so price stops being the conversation.*

The central claim: most businesses compete on price because their offer is comparable to the alternatives. The moment a prospect can line you up against two competitors on a spreadsheet, you are in a commodity market and the only lever left is discounting. A **Grand Slam Offer** is one the prospect cannot comparison-shop — so differentiated in what it includes, what it promises, and who carries the risk that there is no equivalent quote to get.

The practical test we use internally: **could a prospect ask three other Vegas agencies for the same thing?** If yes, it isn't a Grand Slam Offer yet.

Two consequences that matter for us:

1. **Discounting is the weakest available move.** Every dollar off comes straight off the bottom line, it signals the original price was fiction, and it creates a renegotiation cliff when the discount ends. Bonuses and guarantees raise value instead of lowering price, and cost a fraction of their stated worth.
2. **The offer is built from the prospect's obstacles, not from our service list.** Our packages are a menu of what we do. An offer is a list of what stops them from winning, each one removed.

## Core Frameworks

### Market selection comes before offer construction

No offer survives a bad market. The four tests: are they in real pain, do they have money to spend, are they easy to reach, and is the market growing. Getting this wrong is unfixable downstream — a brilliant offer to a market with no money still fails.

**Applied to us:** [[Ideal Client Profile]] v2 already made this call — Vegas home services, $500K–$5M, $2K–$10K/mo marketing spend, owner decides alone. Pain is urgent and seasonal, money is present, they are trivially easy to target (Maps, ad libraries, hiring signals), and Vegas is dense with them. That is a defensible starving crowd. **Note the live tension:** Amber's ad plan targets CA/NV/AZ/UT, which is a different and much wider market than the one this ICP was validated for.

### The Value Equation

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
         ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
                (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

Four levers, and most businesses only push the first one. Raise the dream and the belief; cut the wait and the work. Every element of an offer should be traceable to one of the four.

**The lever we habitually neglect: Perceived Likelihood.** We are good at describing outcomes and bad at making people believe *we specifically* will produce them. That's what case studies, guarantees, and the campus tour are for.

**The lever nobody else in our market pushes: Effort & Sacrifice.** A home services owner's real objection isn't cost, it's "I don't have time for this and I hate being on camera." An offer that visibly removes their labor beats one that's cheaper.

### The offer construction sequence

This is the process the `offer-builder` skill must actually run, in order. Skipping to the stack is the common failure.

1. **Name the dream outcome** in the customer's own words. Not "leads" — *"my trucks are booked into the slow season and I can hire another crew."*
2. **List every obstacle** between them and that outcome. Exhaustively, across before / during / after. Twenty or thirty items, not five. This is the step people rush and it's where all the value comes from.
3. **Invert each obstacle into a solution.** One-to-one. Every problem becomes a thing we do.
4. **Brainstorm delivery vehicles for each solution** — including absurd, expensive, one-to-one versions. What would we do if they paid us $100,000? What if they paid nothing?
5. **Trim.** Cut everything that is high cost to us and low value to them.
6. **Stack** what remains: high perceived value, low marginal cost to deliver. That combination is the whole game.

### The enhancers

Applied *after* the stack exists, never as a substitute for one.

| Enhancer | What it does | Our rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Scarcity** | Limits supply — raises value through fear of loss | Must be **true**. Ours is real: one cyc wall, four shoot days a month, one crew. Never invent a limit. |
| **Urgency** | Puts a clock on the decision | Tie it to something real — the audit expiring, a cohort start, seasonality (Vegas summer). |
| **Bonuses** | Adds value without cutting price | Price each at our **published a-la-carte rate**. Because we have a real menu, our stack values survive scrutiny — most agencies invent theirs. |
| **Guarantees** | Reverses risk — usually the single biggest conversion lever | See taxonomy below. |
| **Naming** | Makes the offer memorable and specific | Avatar + goal + timeframe + a container word. |

### Guarantee taxonomy — ranked by how much we control the outcome

**This ranking is our addition and it is the most important operational rule in this note.** Hormozi lists guarantee types (unconditional refund, conditional performance, anti-guarantee, implied/performance-based). For a service business the useful axis is *control*:

| Tier | We control the outcome | Examples | Real cost to us |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Safe** | Fully | "24 videos and 48 graphics in 90 days or that month is free." "CRM live in 14 days or the build is free." | ~zero if we are operationally competent. Pure credibility. |
| **Strong** | Mostly | "X qualified leads in 90 days or we work free until you get them." | Real but capped. Controllable by choosing verticals we have data for and setting ad spend floors. |
| **Dangerous** | Barely | "10K views." "X new followers." "$X in revenue." | Uncapped. Depends on their close rate, their staff, the algorithm. **Do not offer these.** |

Two standing rules:
- A strong-tier guarantee requires **trailing cost-per-lead data for that vertical**, guaranteed at 60–70% of median so we clear it in a bad month. Guessing the number turns a guarantee into a liability.
- Every strong guarantee carries **client-side obligations** — minimum ad spend, leads answered within 10 minutes during business hours (measurable in GHL), no rejecting leads outside the agreed definition. Without those, we are guaranteeing their operations, not our work.

### Naming

Avatar + goal + interval + container, with a reason the offer exists. "Marketing Package" is not a name. "The 90-Day Busy Season System for Vegas Home Services" is.

## What needs adaptation for an agency

Hormozi's businesses are gyms, supplements, software, and info products. Several things translate badly and the `offer-builder` must account for them:

1. **Our marginal cost is labor, not COGS.** A software bonus costs nothing to duplicate. A shoot day costs a crew and a building. Every stacked item must be checked against **Abril's delivery capacity**, not just against price.
2. **Guarantees cost us delivery months, not refunds.** "We work free until you hit it" means the team services an unpaid account. That is the correct structure — it caps downside at labor and keeps the relationship — but it must be capacity-modeled before it's sold.
3. **Free trials of full service are ruinous at our price point.** 90 days free at Comprehensive = **$10,500 of delivered work per client**. Ten of those alongside 18 paying clients is a 55% capacity increase for zero revenue. Run the arithmetic before any free-service offer reaches a prospect.
4. **Scarcity is genuinely real for us**, which most agencies can't say. Use it. Don't fake it.
5. **We can't infinitely scale a stack.** Trim harder than the book implies.

## Launchpad's uncopyable assets — the raw material for every offer

An offer built on things any agency can do is not a Grand Slam Offer. Ours must be built on these:

1. **The 5,000 sq ft campus** — 20x20 cyc wall, two podcast studios, print shop, all under one roof. Vegas competitors are small teams outsourcing production. This is a physical moat and it also *demonstrates* full-service without ever saying the words.
2. **BrandWithUs** — vehicle wraps, apparel, embroidery, signage in-house. Nobody else bundles a wrapped truck with a marketing retainer. Highest perceived value per dollar of cost we own. *(Blocked: need Jennifer's real materials + install cost and monthly capacity before promising wraps.)*
3. **In-house crew** — Robert, Kyle, Munzelle, Arija, David. Production capacity most competitors rent.
4. **Bilingual delivery** — real advantage in Vegas trades and legal. Proof points: CCS, Toro Taxes.
5. **The Brain and the agent system** — a client-facing AI assistant trained on the client's own business is near-zero marginal cost and nobody in this market offers it.
6. **Anthony himself** — founder access is free to give, capped by choice, and fits the CEO-to-CEO positioning.

## Rules for the `offer-builder` skill

1. Run the six-step sequence in order. Never start at the stack.
2. Obstacle list must exceed 20 items before proceeding.
3. Price every stacked item from [[Packages and Pricing]] — our real published rates only. Never invent a value.
4. Check every stacked item against delivery capacity and flag anything that scales badly.
5. Classify every proposed guarantee by control tier. Refuse to write dangerous-tier guarantees.
6. Any strong-tier guarantee must cite the CPL data it's based on, or be flagged as blocked.
7. Build on at least two uncopyable assets from the list above.
8. Scarcity claims must map to a real constraint.
9. No dollar figure that isn't defensible. Standing rule across all Launchpad output.
10. Never cite Wamen Garage Doors.
